Whenever an encounter check indicates there should be a random encounter while the PCs are traveling or resting outdoors, you can roll on the Travel Encounters table. Each encounter is further detailed below in alphabetical order.

When you roll for an encounter, it can happen at any point in the travel day

If the encounter turns to combat, you can set up the terrain and the distance the PCs start from their enemies as you see fit. For inspiration, you can use the Combat Terrain table.

## Monster from Nearby

The PCs encounter one or more monsters rolled on the monster dungeon table of the monster type that inhabits the closest dungeon. The tables can be found in Monsters.

You determine what the monster is doing when the PCs encounter them, or you can use the Monster Outdoor Behaviors table to determine it.

## Bad Weather

The bad weather event the lasts 24 hours and depends on the habitat where the weather occurs, or the season when it happens if the PCs are in a temperate climate, as shown on the Bad Weather table. Each climate or season has two events on the table. Roll any die. On an odd result, use the first listed event. On an even one, use the second.

Each weather event is detailed below.

### Bad Weather

| Climate or Season | Weather |
| --- | --- |
| Cold or Winter | Blizzard or Cold Snap |
| Desert | Heat Wave or Sandstorm |
| Fall | Rain or Thunderstorm |
| Spring | Rain or Thunderstorm |
| Tropical or Summer | Heat Wave or Thunderstorm |

### Blizzard

During a blizzard, the group’s daily travel speed is reduced by 2 hexes. At the end of each hour a human spends outside without cold weather gear, they make a Strength RR.

**2d10 + S**

**Tier 1:** They take 4d6 P dam.

**Tier 2:** They take 3d6 P dam.

**Tier 3:** They take 2d6 P dam.

A creature takes a cumulative -2 penalty to this test for each time they’ve previously made this test before donning cold weather gear, taking shelter, or experiencing a change in the weather.

Guides, scouts, and trackers take a double bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during the blizzard.

### Cold Snap

During a cold snap, at the end of each hour a human spends outside without cold weather gear, they make a Strength RR.

**2d10 + S**

**Tier 1:** They take 3d6 P dam.

**Tier 2:** They take 2d6 P dam.

**Tier 3:** They take 1d6 P dam.

A creature takes a cumulative -2 penalty to this test for each time they previously made this test before donning cold weather gear, taking shelter, or experiencing a change in the weather.

### Heat Wave

During a heat wave, a if a creature travels more than 2 hexes in a day, they must make a Strength RR.

**2d10 + S**

**Tier 1:** They take 2d6 P dam.

**Tier 2:** They take 1d6 P dam.

**Tier 3:** No effect.

### Rain

In the rain, the group’s travel speed is reduced by 1 hex. Creatures carrying rations must make the following Mind test.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** They lose 1d6 + 1 rations to mold.

**Tier 2:** They lose 1 ration to mold.

**Tier 3:** No effect.

### Sandstorm

During a sandstorm, the group’s travel speed is reduced by 2 hexes. Guides, scouts, and trackers take a double bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during a blizzard.

### Thunderstorm

In a thunderstorm, the group’s travel pace is reduced by 2 hexes. Creatures carrying rations must make the following Mind test.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** They lose 2d6 rations to mold.

**Tier 2:** They lose 1d6 ration to mold.

**Tier 3:** No effect.

Guides, scouts, and trackers take a bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during the thunderstorm.

## Miasma-Touched

Humans who are permanently affected by the Miasma are wicked, but they doesn’t mean they immediately resort to violence when they encounter the PCs. These humans form bonds with others and are capable of showing kindness if they can get something for it. They don’t have a death wish, and therefore don’t seek death.

Roll 1d6 times on the Miasma-Touched Humans table to determine the humans the PCs meet. Then roll on the Miasma-Touched Encounters table to determine their reaction to the PCs.

The reactions on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with Miasmatouched humans so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. A group of bandits might actually be looking for capable recruits instead of treasure. Cultists might have an artifact stashed nearby the PCs can look for. A cannibal might have a refined palate and eat only nobles. The encounter descriptions aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

## Strong Miasma

If the PCs don’t travel in any hexes affected by the Miasma today thanks to an upgraded beacon institution (see Village in *Characters*), then they don’t experience an encounter.

The Miasma the PCs travel and rest in gets stronger for 24 hours. Every PC takes a bane on RRs made to resist the Miasma’s effects and the translucent haze becomes an opaque fog. While this effect lasts, guides, scouts, and trackers take a bane on tests related to their travel role.

If the PCs are lost when this encounter occurs or get lost during it, they are magically transported to a hex that is 1d10 hexes away in a direction of the Ref’s choice.

## Traveler

The PCs encounter 1d10 other humans traveling on the road. The stat blocks for those humans can be rolled on the Travelers table. Then roll on the Traveler Encounters table to determine what makes the encounter noteworthy.

The encounters on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with traveling humans so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. A group of humans who are lost might have a few Miasma touched among them who cause trouble. A group hunting what they believe is an animal could be tracking a deadly monster instead. A group of people searching for treasure might want something the PCs already have. The encounter descriptions aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

### Traveler Reactions

Unless the travelers are in dire straights, they approach others on the road with caution. After all, many of the humans outside the villages are Miasma touched. These travelers keep their guard up until the PCs say or do something that shows they’re kind.

## Wild Animal

Roll for the animal encounter on the appropriate habitat table where the encounter occurs. Then roll an attitude on the Wild Animal Reaction table to determine how they react to the PCs. Some attitude descriptions on the table refer to others, indicated by underlined text.

When the PCs meet a group of animals, it is up to you if all animals have the same attitude or if one, some, or all animals in the group have an individual reaction.

The attitudes on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with animals so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. An asleep animal might be laying on top of something valuable. A treasure-seeking animal might have a hoard of shinies in a nest nearby the PCs can search for. An injured animal might also be territorial, and relax and allow PCs into their territory if their injuries are healed. A protective animal who is also hungry might get distracted from guarding their nest if given food. The attitudes aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

### Wild Animal Reaction

| d100 | Attitude |
| --- | --- |
| 01-05 | **Asleep:** An asleep animal is unconscious when the PCs find them in their path. Waking the animal causes them to become violent. |
| 06-10 | **Attached:** An attached animal becomes inexplicably attracted to a PC randomly chosen by the Ref. The animal follows the PC about and is friendly toward them, but frightened of all other PCs. |
| 11-15 | **Curious:** The animal is curious about what the PCs are and approaches them cautiously but openly. Any aggressive or boisterous behavior causes the animal to become frightened. Calm behavior causes the animal to investigate for a few moments then leave. Generous behavior, such as sharing food with the animal, makes them friendly. |
| 16-20 | **Embattled Animals:** Pick or roll on an encounter table for one or more other animals, humans, or monsters that are battling the animal(s) you originally rolled for. If the players aid the original group, one animal in that group becomes friendly. |
| 21-25 | **Friendly:** A friendly animal happily follows the PCs around for an hour, and a PC can make a test to become the animal’s owner (see Pets in Characters). If the PCs are mean or aggressive to the animal, they flee. |
| 26-35 | **Frightened:** Frightened animals freeze and stare at the PCs, sizing them up. If approached with anything other than the utmost calm and caution, the animal either flees (if they are clearly no match for the PCs and have a way out) or fights until the PCs flee (if they have a chance against the PCs or are cornered). The Ref can allow a Mind test with the Handle Animal skill to attempt to turn the animal’s attitude to curious. |
| 36-45 | **Hungry:** Hungry animals approach when the PCs are eating, drawn by the smell of food. If given at least 1 ration’s worth of food, the animal becomes friendly. If the PCs don’t share their food, the animal attacks, attempting to get some food and run off with it. |
| 46-50 | **Injured:** An injured animal has Stamina equal to half their maximum and is visibly hurt. They are frightened and considered cornered, but any PC who heals them turns their attitude to friendly. |
| 51-55 | **Playful:** Playful animals want to catch sticks, be chased, give a ride, wrestle, or other wise engage in fun with the PCs. They approach in an energetic manor, playing with other animals of their type if any are nearby. If the PCs engage in play with the animal, they become friendly. If not, the animal becomes violent. |
| 56-65 | **Protective:** A protective animal is guarding something and attacks any creature they notice who attempts to get it. These animals stand their ground and fight to the death. Roll any die. On an even result, the animal is defending a nest that has 1d10 babies or eggs (or egg sacks), which can be sold to a village’s stables (each as if it were an individual pet). Otherwise the animal protects one thing on the Minor Treasures table. |
| 66-70 | **Stalking:** Stalking animals attempt to stalk the PCs from hidden positions. They follow the group until one or two PCs are alone and then pounce. It may be that they view the PCs as a threat or food or both. |
| 71-80 | **Starved:** A starved animal is desperate for food, and the PCs have something (or are something) they want to eat. The skinny animal approaches the PCs while displaying aggressive behavior. The animal attacks unless given a day’s worth of food based on their size, at which point they gobble up the food then flee. If the animal is carnivorous, they may attempt to kill a PC and carry their body off if not fed. |
| 81-90 | **Territorial:** Territorial animals don’t want the PCs invading their home. The animals follow the PCs, displaying signs of aggression and never getting too close. As long as the PCs keep moving through the area, the animals stop pursuing them when they exit the hex where the encounter first occurs. Otherwise, the animals attack and fight to the death. |
| 91-95 | **Treasure-Seeking:** Treasure-seeking animals approach when a PC wears or holds an item that attracts the animal. It’s often something shiny or that looks like their food. The animal makes their interest in the item clear, and if the PC doesn’t give it to them, the animal attacks. |
| 96-100 | **Violent:** Violent animals are out for blood, perhaps due to past history with humans or an incurable infection. Whatever the reason, they ambush and attack the PCs. |
