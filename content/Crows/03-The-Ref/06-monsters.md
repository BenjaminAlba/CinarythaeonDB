The term “monster” is used in this game to refer to creatures who are not humans or animals. There are six monster types in the core game:

- **Angels:** Though called angels for their radiance and beauty, these creatures are far from heavenly. They exude magic that charms and warps the minds of their prey.

- **Blood Creatures:** Amalgamations of flesh and fluid of humans and animals, blood creatures are held together by the same magic that makes them ravenous for the flesh of other creatures. Most are animalistic in nature.

- **Demons:** Beasts summoned from the pits of Hell, demons slaughter other creatures for fun. They’re fast, strong, and tenacious.

- **Plants:** Animated by necromancy, these plants use viney tendrils and roots to move, stab, and choke prey to quench a literal bloodthirst.

- **Undead:** The reanimated remains of humans, twisted into killing machines that abhor anything alive.

- **Uniques:** Some monsters in this book are unique experiments of the necromancers. Most have a level of sapience the other monsters lack and there exists only one of each such creature in Cornath.

## Monster Names

Except for uniques, each monster doesn’t have a specific name. Instead, a monster is given a letter after it’s type for referencing in the rules, like “blood creature A” or “undead C.” The arrival of monsters threw the world into chaos, so there is no universal nomenclature for them.

Each monster has art for you to show the players, so they can come up with names for them. There’s a list of suggested names that NPCs might use to refer to a monster so the Ref doesn’t have to come up with them on the fly (but you still can if you want).

## No Darkness Penalty

Monsters have special senses that mean darkness and dim light impose no penalty on them.

## Hates And Likes

Every monster type has likes and hates, objects, other creatures, sights, smells, and sounds that can be used to attract, distract, and misdirect them. They can be used to bait traps and ambushes or allow PCs to sneak by or flee from a threat that’s beyond their capabilities.

### Likes

When a monster encounters a hint of something they like, they seek out the source and investigate further. If the find something they like and no danger is apparent, the monster spends time interacting with it and defend it from threats. If the like is something the creature likes to eat, like people, they will instead devour it or hoard it for later.

If several monsters stumble upon a small amount of something they like, they could fight over which of them gets to keep it.

**All monsters like living, vulnerable prey and the distress sounds created by animals and humans.**

Above all, monsters like living, so they are unlikely to engage with a like while confronted with obvious danger.

### Hates

When a monster encounters something they hate, they hunt down the source and try to destroy it. If a weaker monster (with a power of 9 or lower) finds an overwhelming amount of something they hate, they might flee instead. If a monster hates cheese, they might tear a wheel apart, but would flee from an entire cavern full of dairy delights.

Above all, monsters hate dying, so they are unlikely to engage with a hate while a bunch of crows attack them, however, if one of the PCs is wearing or carrying something they hate, they prioritize attacking that PC.

### Suspicious Circumstances

Monsters can be suspicious of finding something they like or hate in an unusual or dangerous place, such a their favorite food suddenly appearing in the middle of a room or an object they hate hanging over a lava pit.

If a monster approaches something they like or hate in an unusual or dangerous situation, you can have them make this test to see how they react.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** The monster approaches the like or hate without suspicion.

**Tier 2:** The monster approaches with suspicion, investigating anything that seems out of place before approaching the like or hate.

**Tier 3:** The monster doesn’t approach the like or hate, and leaves the area but stays close, waiting to find out what’s really going on and preparing an ambush for potential danger or gathering allies to better investigate.

### Ending the Fight

Monsters are more aggressive than most humans or animals, but they’re still likely to flee from a fight when it becomes clear they’re going to lose. Weaker monsters tend to stick with others of their ilk, and never stray too far from that pack to pursue prey. Stronger monsters pursue prey on their own if they think they can win the fight, but

Monsters in dungeons rarely pursue prey far outside the walls of their home, remaining in their territory to defend it. Monsters wandering the wilds are vulnerable, and tend to flee earlier if a battle starts going south to find safety and way to recover from their wounds.

Monsters will often allow the PCs time to flee after killing a human or pet, provided their kill is large enough to satiate their hunger.

## Monsters as Pets

Some spells and equipment allow the PCs to have a monster as a pet. If a pet monster requires slots, give them a number of slots based on the creature’s size as shown in the Pet Monster Slots table, and then subtract this number from their Stamina maximum. If this would leave the monster with a Stamina maximum of 0 or lower, use the next lowest size category to determine the number of slots they carry instead.

### Pet Monster Slots

| Size | Slots |
| --- | --- |
| Tiny | 0 |
| Small | 2 |
| Medium | 10 |
| Large | 15 |
| Huge | 20 |
| Holy Shit! | 25 |
