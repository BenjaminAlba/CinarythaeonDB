# Pets

> Source: Crows — Character Booklet, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 46–47.

Pets are animals who follow a single human owner. You own pets your buy. You can give a pet you own to another willing human who becomes their owner. If you meet a friendly animal with no owner, you can become their owner with the following test.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** The animal flees from you.

**Tier 2:** The animal follows you at a distance for the next 24 hours, but doesn’t heed your commands. If they take any wounds while following you, they flee. If you use a rest activity to bond with them during this time, you can repeat this test at the end of the rest with an edge.

**Tier 3:** The animal follows you at a distance for the next 24 hours, but doesn’t heed your commands. If they take any wounds while following you, they flee. If you use a rest activity to bond with them during this time, you become the owner when you finish the rest.

### Pet Stats

A pet has backpack inventory slots that can be used to carry equipment for their owner. A pet comes outfitted with a special harness that holds equipment and if the animal is Large or larger, a saddle and other equipment required to ride it. These backpack inventory slots take wounds just like a PC’s.

You can ride any pet who is larger than you. You occupy 6 of a creature’s inventory slots when you ride them.

Pets need to eat each day just like PCs and must eat during a rest to get the benefits of a rest. While traveling in the wild, a pet can hunt and forage for themselves, but in a dungeon or other place with no food sources, they must consume animal feed (the equivalent of rations for them). A Large pet needs twice the amount animal feed to gain the benefits of a rest an avoid starvation, a Huge pet needs four times the amount, and a Holy Shit pet needs eight times the amount.

Pets are not equipment.

### Barding

Pets can wear armor, provided they have the backpack slots to hold it. Determining the cost of barding for a Medium or larger pet requires multiplying the base cost of the armor multiplied by a number found on the Pet Barding table based on the pet’s size. The pet’s size also modifies the number of inventory slots the armor takes up. **Land Vehicles**

Crafting barding works like crafting any other armor, but the crafting goal and number of crafting materials required is increased by an amount equal to the barding’s price modifier.

Enchantments that can be added to suits of armor can be added to barding. There is no additional increase in the enchantment’s price, crafting goal, or materials.
