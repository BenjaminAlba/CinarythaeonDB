# Advancement

> Source: Crows — Character Booklet, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 6–7.

When you return to your village with treasure or equipment recovered from outside of it that isn’t a monster part or pet and wasn’t purchased by your group, crafted by your group, taken from an innocent human, or something that originally belonged to an ally, each PC gains experience points (XP) equal to the item’s total value in gc divided by the number of players. Unique items don’t have a cost. Their XP value is listed on their cards. You also gain XP for items that you recovered and consumed (such as potions) or sold in other villages upon returning to your village.

You keep track of the total XP your PC gains over the course of their adventuring career (called Total XP or TXP), as well as XP you have spent.

You can only spend XP or gain bonuses from TXP when you finish a rest after earning the XP.

You can spend XP on new traits (see Traits). TXP determines your PC’s level.

The Character Level table shows the TXP required for your PC to increase their Effort maximum and characteristics. In addition, **every time you level up, you can increase your Stamina by 2 and gain an expertise.** If you have every expertise in the game, you can instead increase your maximum Stamina by 3.

When you gain a characteristic increase, you can increase one of your characteristics (Agility, Mind, or Strength) by 1, to a maximum of 4. If all of your characteristics are 4, your Stamina maximum instead increases by 2.

## New PC After Death

If your PC dies and you make a new PC to take their place in the same campaign, you can roll on the Backgrounds table an additional number of times equal to the dead PC’s level and choose any background for the new PC from the results you get.

### Starting With More

If all of the other crows in the party have 5,000 or more TXP when you create a new PC, your character lags significantly behind the others in terms of power and survivability. This imbalance is a fun challenge for some players to offset with caution, equipment, and clever tactics. The disparity doesn’t last long. The new PC gains XP fast with experienced crows. For other groups, the new PC is a burden that leaves some folks feeling annoyed or ignored. To offset this unevenness, the Ref can decided that a new PC starts with XP equal to the lowest TXP of a crow already in the party.

It’s unlikely that a new crow will need better equipment than what they start with, since their dead counterpart likely left gear behind that they’ll get, but the Ref can give the PC gold pieces equal to half their starting XP to be used to buy better equipment. This money must be spent on equipment and not upgrading village institutions.
