# The Village

> Source: Crows — Character Booklet, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 48–53.

Crows die and retire, but their village remains. With Cornath recovering from near destruction, your village is one of the only places left for humans to survive. Invest in your village, and your community will thrive and better prepare you for delving into ruins. If you ignore your village, it will decay, leaving you without a place to buy equipment, sell treasure, or safely survive your time outside of dungeons.

In Cornath every village is contained within an enclosed ruin to avoid the Miasma and monsters wandering the wilds. Many of these ruins contain open courtyards, where folks farm or raise livestock, since animals are immune to the Miasma’s effects and only prolonged exposure outdoors poses no threat to a human. Other villages grow mushrooms and live off the meat of animals who thrive in the dark.

## Starting Village

At the start of a campaign, your village institutions include a blacksmith, crypt, general store, inn, and temple. As a group, choose one other institution already established in your village. All these institutions are 1st level. Then come up with a name for your village as a group.

### Village Questions

When you create a village, you can answer the following questions as a group to help flesh out its details and story.

- What old ruin contains the village and keeps out the Miasma?

- Who runs the village? Is there a single ruler, such as a mayor, or a village council?

- What are some common staples that folks in the village eat and drink?

- What is the most important holiday or festival observed by the village?

### NPC Connection

When you create a village or create a new PC that’s part of an existing village, you should create an NPC who lives in that village who is important to your PC in some way. They’re likely a relative, friend, mentor, or someone else who matters a lot to you. This NPC is called a connection since they help link your character to the village. Give this NPC a name, define their relationship to you, and pick one of the following benefits that your connection gives you:

- **Animal Lover:** The connection loves animals, and will look after any of your pets while they are in the village, feeding them for free. Animals that rest with this connection heal 2 additional wounds, or 3 additional wounds if the village’s Prosperity is 6 or higher.

- **Caretaker:** The connection likes to look out for the people they care about. When you rest in the village, you can rest in their home. If you do, you heal 2 additional wounds, or 3 additional wounds if the village’s Prosperity is 6 or higher.

- **Concerned:** This connection is worried about you whenever you leave the village. Once per cycle when you leave the village, they give you 1 torch.

- **Crafty:** The connection has a natural ability to aid in crafting items, though they lack formal training. When you make a crafting roll in the village, you gain a +2 bonus to the roll.

- **Foodie:** This connection loves to make food for their friends. Once per cycle when you leave the village, they provide you with a number of rations equal to half the village’s Prosperity (minimum 1 ration).

- **Magic Enthusiast:** This connection has gathered a lot of knowledge and texts about magic items. Each day, they can examine a number of magic items equal to the village’s Prosperity (minimum 1 magic item) and learn all of its properties and share them with you.

- **Money Bags:** The connection is willing to lend you money. While you’re in the village, you can ask them for a loan of up to 100 × the village’s Prosperity gc (minimum 100 gc). The connection won’t grant you another loan until any previous loan is paid back in full.

- **Monster Collector:** The connection enjoys collecting and trading monster parts. They are willing to trade monster parts you have for ones that they need. They trade 1 monster part of a specific monster type for 5 monster parts of another type.

- **Rival:** This connection is a rival crow. Once per cycle when you leave the village and go to a dungeon, they make a bet with you to see who will have a more monetarily successful adventure away from town. When you return, roll any die. On an even result, you win. On an odd result, they win. The loser pays the winner 50 gc.

- **Smarty Pants:** This connection finds research exciting. While you are in the village, you can ask this connection a question, and the Ref will tell you if the answer is something the NPC can find out through researching in books or by talking to experts in the village. If they can, the connection provides the answer 1 day later. The connection can only research 1 question at a time.

## Village Cycle

Village cycles track when events occur, when new institutions get established and upgraded, and when merchants restock. A village cycle is 20 days long.

## Prosperity

Your village’s Prosperity is a numerical representation of how happy and economically well off the people of your village are. It frequently fluctuates, so don’t panic if it goes down. As institutions improve, so do opportunities for the villagers. The higher the Prosperity, the more villagers care about each other, and seek to protect and help their neighbors. If Prosperity falls, the village’s infrastructure begins to degrade. With less money coming into the town, there are fewer opportunities for villagers, and many become destitute or turn to crime to live.

At the start of a campaign, your village’s Prosperity is 0, which is average. It’s not doing amazing, but it’s not doing terrible. This number is important in determining item availability (see Trade) and the type of events your village experiences (see Village Events).

### Raising Prosperity

There are three ways to raise your village’s prosperity:

1. When you found an institution, you increase your village’s Prosperity by 1.

2. If you upgrade an institution, you increase your village’s Prosperity by 1.

3. If you spend at least 10,000 gc in a village during a cycle on goods from merchant institutions, the village’s prosperity increases by 1. This is usually only done when the PCs are higher level and come back from a dungeon with a butt-load of treasure.

The highest Prosperity a village can have is 10.

### Lowering Prosperity

If a village ends and nothing raised its Prosperity (or would have raised it if the village’s Prosperity started the cycle at 10), its Prosperity decreases by

1. The lowest Prosperity a village can have is -10.

### Village Events

At the end of each village cycle, the Ref rolls for an event that occurs during the next village cyle (which happens whether or not the PCs are in the village). This event impacts the village or the PCs in some way. The higher the village’s Prosperity, the more likely the event is to be a boon to the PCs. The lower the Prosperity, the more likely the event is to have a negative impact.

When a village event occurs, roll on the Village Event table to determine what happens. Unless otherwise specified, when an institution, item, NPC, or PC is referenced by the table, that institution, item, NPC, or PC is randomly chosen by the Ref. If an event involves one or more PCs directly, it occurs the next time they are in the village.

## Trade

You can buy or trade goods in your village, provided you have merchants who can supply them. A merchant’s description in Institutions lists what the buy and sell.

### Buying

Merchants provide certain goods and services based on their level. If you want to buy the best equipment from a merchant, you must increase their level to the maximum. See Institutions for more information.

Unless otherwise noted, items are purchased for the price listed in the rules, either in a booklet or on their cards.

If the PCs want to buy more than one of an item a merchant carries that costs 100 gc or more, the percent chance that the merchant carries two of the item you want is 10 × the village’s prosperity (minimum 10% chance). If there are two of the item and the PCs want to buy a third or more, the percent chance of subsequent copies of the item being in stock decreases by 10%. Merchants restock at the start of each cycle. At the Ref’s discretion, the same rules apply to items with a value lower than 100 gc.

### Selling

Items can be sold in the village to an appropriate merchant. The money gained from the sale is a percentage of the item’s base cost based on the village’s Prosperity, as shown on the Sale Percentages table.

**Sale Percentages**

| Prosperity | Sale Percentage |
| --- | --- |
| -10 | 30% |
| -9 to -6 | 40% |
| -5 to -2 | 45% |
| -1 to 1 | 50% |
| 2 to 5 | 55% |
| 6 to 9 | 60% |
| 10 | 70% |

## Village Crafting

Some institutions, called artisans, provide crafting services for PCs. If the PC can’t outright buy a craftable item they want, they can pay the institution to do it.

- To begin the project, the institution must be given the crafting materials for the item and payment, which is the item’s full price minus the cost of the crafting materials.

- The institution needs 1 day to craft complexity 1 items. They can craft other items in a number of days equal to 1d6 × the item’s complexity.

- If you pay twice as much, the institution makes it a rush job and the time to craft the item is halved.

- When the item is complete, you can visit the institution and pick it up.
