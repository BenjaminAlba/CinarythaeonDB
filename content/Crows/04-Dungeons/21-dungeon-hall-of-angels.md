# Dungeon: Hall of Angels

> Source: Crows — Dungeons, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 37–45. Original playtest wording retained.

*This dungeon is a fun challenge with a chance of death for three to five 7th-9th level characters.*

An unparalleled performer and a master of illusion, Zaziel the Cunning ruled from a great temple of trickery where—according to legend— only the cleverest could reach them. They watched, dismayed, as the Necromancers fell one by one, layering their temple with traps that would punish the wicked even as they hoped to save the Necromancers from their greed.

The angels maintain the temple, now called the Hall of Angels, and seek the voice of the Great Light through one powerful angel. Those clever crows who overcome Zaziel’s final deceptions can find within treasures worthy of divinity.

## Hall Arrival

When the PCs reach the Hall of Angels, they notice the following details:

- A great white temple sprawls amid the Rusted Mountains, bathed in sunlight and shining from within.

- Most of the temple is flat, though one tower lies at the center, holding a blazing fire that casts light throughout the valley. The light is painful to look at and scalds the land it washes over.

- While most of the temple is in pristine condition, some of the quartzite walls are weathered and fractured.

The lit areas are depicted on the map. A PC who enters the light during their approach takes 1d6 P dam and alerts the angels to intruders, as the light is coming from the **angel J** in area 4.

## Entering the Hall

The PCs can enter the Hall of Angels in any of the following ways:

- They can enter the front doors or climb over the wall into area 1.

- They can climb over the walls into area 1.

- They can climb up area 4’s tower and descend straight into the temple.

- They can enter the hallways through the crumbling walls on the west or east side of the temple.

When the PCs enter the Hall of Angels, the first Dungeon Turn begins. Let them know, and start the 30-minute timer.

### Shared Details

Unless otherwise noted, interior areas of the Hall of Angels share the following details:

- **Light:** The interior areas of the hall are brightly lit. There is no darkness to be found within.

- **Floor Heights:** Unless otherwise noted, the ceiling of the temple is 50 feet high.

- **Map:** Areas of the hall are keyed to the Hall of Angels map.

- **Secret Passages:** Some rooms and halls are connected via secret passages. A Medium or smaller creature can squeeze through a secret passage. If the PCs search specifically for a secret near a passage’s entrance, they find it. Otherwise, searching for a secret passage requires the following test:

**2d10 + M**

The EN for the Hall of Angels is 3. If the PCs stepped into the light during the approach, the EN increases by 1. If anyone in the party is carrying *Zaziel’s Book of Elation* from area 7, the EN increases by 2. When a random encounter occurs, roll on the Angel Encounters table in *The Ref*.

## 1. Entrance

- This area is an open courtyard exposed to the outside.

- Ornate relief sculptures ornament the entrance, capturing wild shapes in motion. Feathered and animalistic creatures, beings with multiple arms and elongated limbs, all of them in glorious revelry underneath a great sun.

- Inside is a gorgeous white quartzite courtyard filled with elegant colonnades surrounding a rectangular pool that reflects the surroundings.

- Brittle yet colorful foliage grows in abundance around the far doors, which open into a dark room.

### Entrance Trap

The entrance is enchanted with powerful illusion spells meant to eternally ensnare creatures who try to enter the Hall of Angels. If the PCs walk around the pool and through the doors at the north end, they seem to walk into another courtyard—in actuality, they walk back into this same courtyard through the south doors.

The area holds several hints as to the trap:

- The surroundings are exactly the same. The northern doors are always slightly ajar in the same way, leading into the dark.

- After the first time they pass through the northern doors, there are differences between the surroundings and the pool’s reflection each time they leave. The reflection shows bright light through the open doors, as well as statues of feathered divine figures on either side of it.

- The PCs’ reflections in the pool change each time they pass through the door, gradually appearing more angelic—beautiful, twisted, elongated, entrancing.

A PC who searches the entrance courtyard can make the following test, automatically getting a tier 3 result if they ask to specifically search the pool’s reflections.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No info | The PC notices that their reflection in the pool is changing | The PC notices that the area looks different in the pool. The room beyond the northern door is lit, and the door is decorated with divine figures. |

The PCs trigger an encounter check each time they pass through the northern doors and reenter at the southern doors.

### Leaving the Entrance

The PCs have several ways to leave the trap:

- If the PCs jump into the pool where the lit open doors are reflected (as if they’re jumping “into” the door), they’re transported to area 2.

- Leaving through the southern takes the PCs back outside the temple, where they can break in through one of the other entrances.

- The PCs can climb over the walls to the outside of the temple. Doing this puts them in the light (see Hall Arrival).

- A secret passage behind the foliage leads to an interior hallway.

## 2. Monument to Joy

- Paintings of celebration and performance cover the walls of this interior chamber, which is made of gleaming white quartzite.

- Six angel statues loom over the room, three on each side, holding blazing braziers of bright white light as if to flood the room with it. The bodies are stone, but the eyes gleam, entirely white.

- In the middle of the room is a statue of Zaziel, their body captured in vibrant motion, as if they’re painting the air with magic. The base of the statue is inscribed with the words, “Make an offering to the Great Light,” and an offering bowl lays before it.

- The statue is silhouetted by the largest of the fires, casting their performance in burning radiance.

- The doors to the north are sealed tightly shut. Music faintly sounds from behind them.

### Offering

The northern doors are sealed until an offering worth at least **25 gc** is made.

If the PCs leave the offering in the offering bowl, the statues’ eyes flare and harmonious chords thunder within the room as burning radiance washes over it. The PCs take as 3d6 P dam and six **angel A** emerge from the braziers and attack.

If the PCs instead give the offering to the fire behind it, the northern doors open.

### Secret Passage

A secret passage leading to the hallway is hidden along the eastern wall of the room, where a switch to open the door is disguised along the detailed paintings.

### Treasure

The statues’ eyes are all pure quartz (**gem worth 50 gc**). All 12 eyes can be pried from the statues.

## 3. Hall of Beauty

- This room is flooded with two pure, resounding voices intertwining in harmony.

- The song bounds off many corpses suspended from the ceiling at different heights. Bodies— dozens of bodies of thieving crows made beautiful with feathers, extra limbs, and animal features, then encased in gold. Their twisted forms are reflected on the polished quartzite walls.

- The singing comes from within, where two beautiful humanoids pour molten gold over another unfortunate victim.

- Large ornate doors on the far side of the room are sealed with an arcane sigil.

- Two hallways lead north, and the east and west walls each have their own hallway.

If a creature enters the room without sneaking, they draw the attention of **angel H** and **angel I**, who curated this collection and guard the way to area 4.

### Angels

The angels display fervor for both beauty and violence. They prolong fights, enjoy agony, and immortalize suffering.

If the PCs are noticed, combat can be avoided under two circumstances:

- A PC can give them an item or performance of pure beauty. The angels have quite an alien definition of beauty, however—it must be passionate and striking. They would deride a peaceful painting of wildflowers yet praise a deadly combat or an item with a bloody history. Even if the item or performance isn’t violent, it must have powerful emotions behind it.

- A PC can give them Zaziel’s eyes from area 8 or Zaziel’s heart from area 12.

If both angels are near death, they merge into **angel G** to call reinforcements.

### Treasure

The angels have six gold bars (each a **Tiny art object worth 500 gc**), a **necklace of teeth**, and a dozen organs from demons (**creature parts**).

## 4. Sanctuary of Light

- Light. Music. The two weave together in this massive hall, the quartzite shining brilliantly and the hall swelling with harmonies that are barely comprehensible to mortal ears.

- The quartz seems barely able to hold the light cast by the being floating high in the room—a magnificent light swallowed by elongated arms, an all-seeing eye seeming to peel you apart.

- Around the eye float small lights, while lionlike humanoids overcome by light prowl the sanctuary’s floor, their movements erratic as they watch hungrily.

- They weave between piles of abounding treasure—gold, statues, a circlet, books.

### Angels

The **angel J** is the Voice of the Light, an angel who claims to hear the voice of the Great Light. Those in the Hall of Angels believe the Great Light will return to Cornath and save it from its wretchedness, and the Voice leads them in punishing the wicked and turning minds and flesh toward the light.

The angel J is attended by six **angel A** and four **angel B**.

If the PCs approach without malice, the Voice turns an eye to their souls. Even if the PCs fail the test against the angel’s Blinding Light, they hear the angel’s voice—reverberating with a thousand voices in ecstasy—as they say, “Yield mind, flesh, or life.”

The Voice shares the following information if asked:

- **The Great Light:** The angels here await the return of the Great Light to Cornath. Zaziel alone must rule it.

- **Other Angels:** The angels here serve one purpose. Having witnessed the fall of the Necromancers alongside Zaziel, they wish to purge the world of its greed and wickedness.

- **The Hall of Angels:** Zaziel ruled from this temple and turned it into a temple of trickery and illusion. Only the most cunning would find their way to audience with them.

When the PCs have asked their questions, the Voice says again, “Yield mind, flesh, or life.” The other angels join in, demanding simultaneously: Yield mind, flesh, or life. They demand the PCs either surrender their free will in service to Zaziel, submit their flesh and accept transformation into the angel, or fight to the death.

### Sanctuary

The ceiling is 70 feet high. The tower is 200 feet high and opens high above the temple.

### Treasure

The Sanctuary of Light has **10,525 gc**, a white diamond circlet (**Small art object worth 4,000 gc**), four clear quartz statutes of multilimbed, feathered beings (each a **Small art object worth 1,000 gc**), and **Zaziel’s Book of Elation.**

## 5. Chamber of Self-Reflection

- Every wall in this chamber is lined with mirrors of varying sizes.

- At the far end of the room, a treasure hoard glitters in the mirrors’ reflections.

### Mirror Trap

No matter where the PCs look, their reflections always watch them. If the PCs approach the treasure, their reflections begin moving strangely, prowling animalistically toward the glass.

If a PC comes within 1 square of the treasure hoard, their reflection—now twisted and inhuman—emerges from the glass and attacks.

The reflections use the statistics of an **undead A** but should be narrated to copy the PCs’ weapons, abilities, and behaviors.

### Treasures

The treasure hoard includes **2,148 gc**, a diamond ring (**Tiny art object worth 2,500 gc**), and a note that says, “Seek the vaults where treasures lies.”

## 6. Chamber of Dread

- The quartz walls are nearly translucent in this room, soaking in light from the braziers burning white flames.

- Two statues face a hoard of treasure. Their sinuous features wind up to their faces, where their mouths are wrenched open mid-scream.

When the PCs come more than two squares into this room, the statues unleash a piercing scream and attack. Each is an **angel D** hiding in plain sight.

In the quartz walls of this room, the angels’ reflections look like the PCs’ greatest tangible fear. While they’re in this room surrounded by their fears, PCs take a bane on the Mind RR made against the angels’ Harmonic Trance.

### Treasure

The treasure hoard contains **1,452 gc**, a **life drain spellbook**, and two bottles with preserved animal eyes (**creature parts**).

## 7. Chamber of Laughter

- Along the back quartzite wall is a massive tapestry depicting Zaziel in moments of great performance. They throw magic into splendorous display, create spectacle from nothing, and entertain Cornath from mountain to sea.

- Displayed before the tapestry is a glass case atop a wooden pedestal. A thick arcane tome lies within, worn and well used. The inscription reads, “*Zaziel’s Book of Elation.* Light seeks light.”

- The floor around the case is littered with tiny white objects.

### Pedestal

There are two layers to the trap on this pedestal. A fake inscription gets them into position (next to the pedestal) for the real trap to strike. The pedestal is surrounded by brittle bone and teeth fragments from incinerated crows, hinting at the real trap.

A PC who examines the pedestal notices small arcane runes carved into the wood and can decipher their meaning with the following test.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No info | The runes mean “lift together.” | The runes, which mean “lift together,” are magical but not dangerous. |

When three or more PCs come within 2 squares of the pedestal, they activate a hidden pressure plate underneath the pedestal. The pedestal explodes in burning radiant heat, and each creature within 5 squares of it must make an **Agility RR**.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4d6 dam | 2d6 dam | No damage |

A PC killed by this trap is incinerated into ash, leaving nothing but bone and enamel fragments.

The cabinet contains the book, **692 gc**, and a **phasing potion**. The tapestry is a **Medium art object worth 500 gc**.

### The Book of Elation

Zaziel’s Book of Elation is a powerful spellbook worthy of an archmage. Unfortunately, this is a fake (but you can give players the real card). While a PC carries this fake spellbook, angels are drawn to it, raising the dungeon’s EN by 1.

The spellbook claims to have the *greater phantasm*, *light shell*, and *illusory double* spells, but if a PC tries to cast from it, the air rings with laughter and they take 1d6 P dam of psychic backlash.

A PC who inspects the book can make the following test.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The PC finds nothing, and they take 2d6 P dam as quiet laughter cackles around them while they try to focus on the words. | The PC knows the book is fake, but takes 1d6 P dam before realizing it. | The PC knows this book is a fake. |

The real copy of this book is in area 4.

## 8. East Treasure Vault

This room can only be accessed if the PCs discover the secret passage in area 9 before the rooms collapse.

- The entirety of this room is taken up by a looming statue, its torso leaning heavily onto its arms as its head hangs over the room. Curly hair pours over it in waves.

- Its eyes are almost closed, its fingers digging into the floor of the temple almost violently, as if it imposed on this space rather than being built inside it.

- Its lips never move, but the sound of crying echoes through the chamber. Sometimes it peels through the room like tears of laughter before plunging into the wracked sobs of agony.

- Liquid drips from both of its eyes: green liquid from one eye, red from the other.

- Two small spheres lay amid its arms, as if it’s curled around them protectively.

A PC who can produce a container can bottle the statue’s tears. The red liquid is a **rage potion** and the green liquid is a **hallucinogenic poison**. There are enough tears for one vial of each.

The spheres are holy recreations of Zaziel’s eyes.

## 9, 10, 11. Chambers of Loyalty

These rooms can only be entered if three creatures enter them at the same time.

- Three closed quartz doors stand, pale and larger than other doors within this temple. Each one bears a handprint underneath runes.

- The left doors says “Showmanship” and has a left handprint. The middle door says “Misdirection” and has a right handprint. The right door says “Presentation” and has two handprints, left and right, side by side.

To enter the room, creatures must press their hands into the handprints simultaneously, which causes the doors to open for only seconds before they close again.

When the PCs enter the rooms, share these details, which apply to each room:

- On the far wall is an hourglass. Sand starts streaming down quickly, settling in hushed sounds at the bottom.

- In the center of the room, a statue of a multilimbed, winged figure stretches out a dozen arms, each holding a gem. Stone groans as its mouth slowly opens and a voice of harmonious chords says, “Find your treasure. Time is ticking.”

- Around the statue lie other precious objects.

### Statue Treasures

Each statue has twelve hands that hold a **gem worth 100 gc**. Each room also has an angelic statue (**Small art object worth 1,500 gc**), a **star diamond bar**, **fine enchanter’s tools**, and a crystalline hourglass (**Tiny art object worth 250 gc**).

However, the treasures in areas 9 and 11 are illusions that disappear when removed from the room. Only the treasures in 10 are tangible and can be grabbed.

### Hourglass

The hourglass runs out at the end of the DT after the PCs enter one of these rooms for the first time. If the PCs are inside the room when they run out, they must make an **Agility RR** as the rooms collapse, forcing them out.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6d6 dam | 3d6 dam | No damage |

This also closes the ways to areas 8 and 12, sealing off their treasure.

### Angels

The secret passages in areas 9 and 11 each hide an **angel D**, who attempts to use Harmonic Trance to convince the PC that the PC(s) in area 10 will betray them—they have the real treasures, after all.

If a PC fails, they’re to attack any PCs in area 10 and take the real treasures for themselves.

## 12. West Treasure Vault

This room can only be accessed if the PCs discover the secret passage in area 11 before the rooms collapse.

- The entirety of this room is taken up by a fallen statue, its ribcage ripped violently open, its stone ribs splintering outward, its back arched as if suspended in anguish and pain.

- Its eyes are wide with fear, its arms reaching out for salvation, as if it fell from grace into this room rather than being built inside it.

- Its lips never move, but the sound of shrieking echoes through the chamber. Sometimes it thunders through the room like shrieks of joy before descending into screams of agony.

- A massive greatsword is stabbed into its chest, around which sprawls a pile of treasure.

- Something withered and black lays at the center of its ribcage.

The statue’s chest contains **1,546 gc**, two clear diamond teardrops (**gem worth 2,000 gc**), and a **greatsword with the flaming** property.

The withered object is a holy recreation of Zaziel’s heart.

## 13. Chamber of Flame

- Stark white light bleaches the quartz walls and radiant heat scorches the air from a roaring wall of white fire in the middle of the room.

- Several humanoid corpses stand within them, bodies contorted in pain, their burnt remains grotesque remnants of suffering.

- Beyond the fire gleams a bookshelf of spellbooks, the flames licking at their edges.

### Fire

The fire is 1 square wide and stretches from the west wall to the east. The flames are illusory and don’t harm anyone who enters them.

### Angels

As soon as the PCs enter the room, they’re attacked by four **angel B** made invisible by illusion magic. The PCs are safe while standing in the flames—the angels avoid the radiant flames that have consumed them and don’t attack. If a PC leaves the flame, they resume their assault until killed.

A PC can determine where an angel is by making the following test to track them as a maneuver. They can point out angels to others as part of the same maneuver.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The PC notices no angels. | The PC notices one angel. | The PC notices two angels. |

### Tomes

The bookshelf holds a **cacophony spellbook**, an **invisibility spellbook**, and a **pain book**.

## 14. Chamber of Charity

- The air is thick with blood and ashes from the fires blazing at the corners of a table and the injured woman bound to it.

- Her torso bears many gouges, her ankle is twisted at a sharp angle, and her limbs look malnourished.

- She is young, with porcelain features and pale hair. Her head lolls to the side weakly as she rasps, “Please. I hurt. I’m so hungry.”

### Injured Woman

The young woman claims to be a crow and begs the PCs to help her with the following problems:

- Deep gashes in her torso need bandaging to stop the bleeding.

- Her ankle is twisted and needs a splint.

- The angels took her supplies.

- She’s starving.

In reality, this is an **angel C** disguised as a young human woman. She calls herself Ilia—a name she stole from a crow she killed—and punishes greedy, selfish crows who deny her help and food.

The crows can help Ilia to gain treasures from her. However, just 1 ration isn’t enough to satisfy her. She keeps begging for food until given 3 rations.

If the crows refuse her (either the first time or when she asks for more rations), she gives them three chances. First, she says, “Please, I’m so hungry.” Then, crawling forward, her jaw unhinging, she says, “I’m so hungry, I’m so hungry.” Finally, clawing at them with elongated limbs and claws, her massive mouth swinging open, she screams, “I’m hungry!” If the crows still won’t give her food, she attacks. She can make a Bite attack that uses the same statistics as her Claw attack (it’s just narratively grosser).

### Ilia’s Treasure

If the crows help Ilia, she gives them one item from the Minor Treasures table in *The Ref* per problem they solved. She gives them an item from the Major Treasures table if they solve all her problems.

She also warns them about the angels in area 3, saying they’re immensely violent unless shown something beautiful. Still, she warns that it can’t be a quiet beauty—it must ignite the heart. She also mentions they’re devout worshipers of the Great Light and can be swayed by objects personal to Zaziel.

## 15. Chamber of Gifts

- Most of this room is overtaken by a massive stone statue of a five-armed angelic figure.

- Their head is tilted back, eyes closed and lips parted. Four of their enormous hands are outstretched in each corner of the room. The back two hands are held slightly higher than the front two hands.

- The fifth hand is lifted high toward the ceiling, something shiny glinting on it.

- Before their bare torso is a table carrying four mundane objects: a rope, a smiling theater mask, a thorned rose, and a looking glass. Beside them lies a parchment.

### Object Puzzle

The note on the table reads:

*The world said: What good is a deceiver?*

*And the great deceiver said: Look.*

*And they offered four gifts:*

*Four certainties*

*And four lies.*

*The great deceiver held high their right hand*

*And said: Look,*

*Your eyes search here*

*And my hand hides there*

*A clever trick.*

*You succumb.*

*The first gift: Laughter.*

*The great performer held high their left hand*

*And said: Look,*

*I hold the lens*

*I show you parts of you*

*You never want to see.*

*You turn away.*

*The second gift: Truth.*

*The great illusionist held out their right hand*

*And said: Look,*

*I perform and your chest fills*

*Joy—sorrow—aching—pouring from your ribs.*

*You bleed it.*

*The third gift: Love.*

*The great liar held out their left hand*

*And said: Look,*

*How evil this world*

*Come, and laugh, and dance*

*And forget.*

*You hide.*

*The last gift: Escape.*

*The world said: What good is a performer?*

*And the great performer said: So it is, after all.*

*Look not. Feel not.*

*Seek your treasure.*

*But blame not the liar for his truths.*

Each object represents one gift:

- The theater mask represents laughter.

- The looking glass represents truth.

- The thorned rose represents love.

- The rope represents escape.

At first glance, the theater mask should be set on the high right hand, the looking glass on the high left hand, the thorned rose on the low right hand, and the rope on the low left hand. (This refers to the statue’s left and right, not the directions when facing it.)

However, there’s another layer to this puzzle: The objects are enchanted with illusion magic to look and feel like each other. The poem hints at this by calling them “four lies” and implying the puzzle’s solution in the last stanza.

The PCs can’t trust their sight or touch. Rather, they must rely on other senses—smell, taste, the sound the objects make when struck—to determine which object is which.

The objects are actually arranged as follows:

- The thorned rose is the theater mask and should be set on the high right hand.

- The theater mask is the looking glass and should be set on the high left hand.

- The rope is the thorned rose and should be set on the low right hand.

- The looking glass is the rope and should be set on the low left hand.

If the PCs seem stuck, a PC trying to solve the puzzle can make the following test to realize part of the puzzle:

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each object should be placed on one of the statue’s open hand. | Each object represents one of the gifts, but the poem also emphasizes that the narrator is a liar. | The poem focuses on the senses when it says to seek treasure: “Look not. Feel not.” There may be other senses that can solve this puzzle. |

### Statue

If the PCs place the four objects on the wrong hands, statue attacks. It uses the statistics of **angel F**, except it doesn’t shed light and its arms can cover the entire room. It always keeps its fifth arm out of reach.

### Treasure

If the PCs solve the puzzle, the statue lowers its fifth arm, offering a palm full of treasure. The treasure includes **2,202 gc** and a dozen small pearls (**gems worth 100 gc**).

A PC who tries to climb the fifth arm to reach the treasure without solving the puzzle must make the following test:

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The statue grabs the PC with one of its other arms and crushes them. The PC takes 4d6 dam. | The PC reaches the top of the hand and can grab one of the treasures of their choice. Then the statue grabs and throws them. The PC takes 2d6 dam. | The PC can climb up, loot the treasure, then climb safely back down while avoiding the statue. |

If the statue is defeated, the PCs can take the treasure without issue.
