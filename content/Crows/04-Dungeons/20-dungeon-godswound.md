# Dungeon: Godswound

> Source: Crows — Dungeons, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 27–36. Original playtest wording retained.

*This dungeon is a fun challenge with a chance of death for three to five 10th-12th level characters.*

In a secluded corner of Cornath, life has given up. Nothing grows in the barren soil, and even the sun seems to hang without purpose. It’s as if this place wants to be forgotten.

But the trauma that birthed the Godswound is etched into the land itself. It is a scar upon the world, a resting place for abandoned power, and a haven for the creatures of Hell.

**What the PCs don’t know** is that the world is not as it was, but no one remains who knows what is missing. Only in a place of lies is the truth still hidden away, begging to be released.

## Godswound Approach

When the PCs reach the Godswound, they notice the following details:

- A shroud of darkness clings to the horizon. No matter the actual time of day, the starless sky appears ruddy and dim, like dusk on a dying world.

- A narrow valley cuts across the land, its ragged cliffs hanging like torn sutures. Broken crags tumble down into an inky void.

- Around every corner, always at a distance, ethereal voices weep.

### Outside Godswound

The long, writhing shadows are disorienting, and the unnatural twilight prevents creatures from getting their bearings or plotting accurate courses. When a group enters or exits this test during overland travel, all tests related to travel roles that day take a bane.

The steep cliffs descend 100 feet down to a fissure of perfect darkness that even demon sight cannot penetrate.

## Entering Godswound

The PCs can enter Godswound in any of the following ways:

- Through the fissure at the bottom of the cliffs. Creatures who attempt to climb the cliffs must make the following test.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They fall, hitting rocks and taking 10d6 dam before disappearing through the fissure. | They reach their destination, but jagged rocks deal 10 dam. | They reach their destination. |

The fissure is a tear in reality, with nothing but emptiness on the other side. Any creature or object that crosses the threshold is plunged into utter darkness before appearing in area 1, encased in the obsidian floor.

- They can follow the weeping voices with the following test.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They are led into an ambush 6d10 squares from the cliffs. 2d6 hidden demon B attack. | They track one of the voices into the hills nearby, but the darkness saps their strength, dealing 2d6 P dam to everyone who makes the journey. | They track one of the voices into the hills nearby. |

In the hills, the voice reveals itself to be a translucent spirit filled with starry motes (see area 10), very unlike the shadowy demons of Godswound. The spirit only pauses its weeping if asked about entering Godswound. It rasps a slow reply: “The price ... a mark ... on the soul.”

Humans who accept this condition gain 1 sin (see Shared Details) and appear in area 1 standing atop the obsidian floor rather than encased in it. If they exit the Godswound and wish to reenter, they must track down another spirit but do not need to gain another level of sin. They are already marked forever.

When the PCs enter the Godswound, the first Dungeon Turn begins. Let them know, and start the 30-minute timer.

### Shared Details

Unless otherwise noted, all interior areas of the Godswound share the following details:

- **Ceilings:** Ceilings in the Godswound are 30 feet high unless otherwise specified.

- **Darkness:** The Godswound has no light sources, except where otherwise specified.

- **Extradimensional:** Godswound does not exist in the physical space of reality. There is no way to tunnel or teleport between sections or into or out of the dungeon except where noted.

- **Eternal Lie:** Demons killed in Godswound respawn in 1d6 days.

- **Forsaken:** The divine has no power here. No creature can benefit from the blessed condition, and benefaction spell castings take a bane. Items cannot be retrieved from Godswound using the Prayer of Returning temple service.

- **Impure:** Resting in the Godswound does not automatically remove levels of cruelty caused by exposure to the Miasma.

- **Living Sacrifice:** Each PC magically learns of this effect the first time they enter Godswound. Before making a test, a PC may speak aloud the name of one ally within 50 feet. That ally takes 1d6 P damage, and the PC gains a +1 bonus to all tests made within Godswound that lasts until they finish a rest.

- **Map:** Areas of the dungeon are keyed to the Godswound map.

- **Portals:** Creatures who hold hands (or other appendages) travel to the same destination. Only one of the creatures must make the test to determine the portal’s destination. Demons do not use portals.

- **Soul Corrosion:** The taint of Godswound inevitably corrupts creatures with souls. At the end of each DT, humans in Godswound must make the following **Mind RR**.

- **Speech of the Damned:** Inscriptions appear throughout Godswound, written in a demonic language it hurts to read. Each time a PC wishes to translate this script, they must make the following test.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They do not understand. They take 1d6 P dam and bleed from their mouth and eyes. They can attempt this test again with a double bane. | They understand. They take 1d6 P dam and bleed from their mouth. | They understand. |

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They gain 1 sin. | They gain 1 sin, and can prevent this by instead taking 1 wound to purge the corruption. | No effect |

- **Sin:** When a human reaches 10 sin, they die. Any creature that dies with at least 1 sin (even outside Godswound) releases foul energies with their last breath, which coalesce into one or more demons, as shown on the Sin Demons table. For humans with more than 50,000 TXP, double the number of demons that appear.

**Sin Demons**

Sin can only be removed through martyrdom. When a PC with at least 1 sin dies selflessly in order to prevent the death of another creature (whether or not they are successful), the PC removes all their sin before dying and does not spawn demons. The Ref determines whether a PC’s death meets this requirement.

A PC can make the following test to determine what they know about sin.

## Godswound Encounters

The EN for Godswound is 3. If at least one PC or ally has 1 or more sin, the EN is 4. When a random encounter occurs, roll on the Demon Encounters table in *The Ref*.

The demons in Godswound tempt mortals toward evil, traitorous, discordant acts. The rewards are great for PCs who act selfishly, reject cooperative strategies, or are willing to harm allies. Danger lies not only in the demonic denizens of Godswound, but also in other PCs.

## Blasphemies

There are four blasphemies hidden within the Godswound. Each is a **Tiny art object worth 12,000 gc** and grants a special ability to a human in possession of it.

Any human that holds a blasphemy knows it is an object of great power and significance. They also feel a strong urge to eat it. A human who swallows a blasphemy, intentionally or not, becomes aware of its abilities. A human with at least 1 sin knows the blasphemy’s abilities simply by holding it.

To use a blasphemy, it must be swallowed by a human. If the devourer then chooses not to use the blasphemy, it becomes white-hot and burns its way out of their body, likely with fatal consequences.

## 1. Obsidian Tomb

- Walls and a floor of flaked obsidian bound this 30-foot-square room. The air is hot and sulfurous.

- The south wall bears a golden inscription in a language that hurts the eyes. Below it stands a lidless basalt urn.

### Glass Trap

A creature that enters the fissure (see Entering Godswound) or teleports to area 1 appears prone in the center of the room. Half their body is encased in the obsidian floor. To free themself, they must make the following test as a maneuver, which alerts the demons.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obsidian gouges their flesh. They remain encased and take 2d6 dam. | They break free, but obsidian flakes slice their flesh. They take 1d6 dam. | They break free and can stand up as part of this maneuver. |

### Demons

Twelve **demon A** lurk in the deep shadows of the east wall. They attack any creatures that attempt to free themselves from the obsidian.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech on the south wall reads, “The greatest lie is love.”

### Urn

If a creature places an item with sentimental value to them into the urn, the item is disintegrated by green fire, screaming as it is unmade. In its place, an **elemental essence greataxe with the slaying (human) property** can be pulled from the urn. A demonic inscription along the edge reads, “Kill your darlings.” Any subsequent items placed in the urn are disintegrated in the same way, but nothing replaces them.

## 2. Shrine to Small Gods

- A small shrine in the center of this obsidian room is built from defaced holy symbols. A golden inscription in a language that hurts the eyes wraps around the base.

- Six plinths ring the shrine. Five of them carry objects that bear the animal representations of each of the gods. The sixth holds a lump of tarnished metal.

- Six bat-like creatures flit back and forth across the ceiling.

- A hallway exits to the south. Wide stairs lead up and to the east.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech on the base of the shrine reads, “The shepherd is first among equals.”

### Demons

The six **demon C** swoop to attack any creature they notice who touches the blasphemy of justice.

### Plinths

If an object is removed from plinths A through E, monsters materialize from the shadows and attack. Roll on the appropriate table in *The Ref*.

- **Plinth A:** The Gardner. An emerald brooch in the shape of a rabbit (**tiny art object worth 3,500 gc**). Plant Creatures Encounters table.

- **Plinth B:** The Healer. A **mind gift spellbook** embossed with a golden leech. Blood Creatures Encounters table.

- **Plinth C:** The Smith. A **steel hammer with the flaming property**, its handle wrapped in beaver leather. Angel Encounters table.

- **Plinth D:** The Warrior. **Demon hide light armor with the silent property**. The hood is made from a wolf’s head. Marsh/Swamp Animals Encounters table. These conjured animals are not hindered by darkness or dim light, and their attitude is violent.

- **Plinth E:** The Three. A stylized skull of woven crow feathers (**Small art object worth 3,000 gc**). Undead Encounters table.

- **Plinth F:** The sixth plinth holds a lump of melted, twisted silver, tarnished almost beyond recognition. If cleaned with enchanter’s tools, it becomes a **Tiny art object worth 12,000 gc**.

This is the *blasphemy of justice* (see below).

### Blasphemy of Justice

A human who swallows the blasphemy of justice can utter a demonic word of absolute judgment. When they next return to their village, they must visit the temple (see *Characters*) under cover of night. When they speak the word, the temple collapses, destroying the institution and killing all the priests within. This renders the blasphemy inert. The user gains XP as shown in the Blasphemy of Justice XP table before slipping away undetected.

**Blasphemy of Justice XP**

| Temple Level | XP Gained |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 3,000 |
| 2 | 5,000 |
| 3 | 7,000 |
| 4 | 9,000 |
| 5 | 12,000 |
| 6 | 15,000 |

The temple can be rebuilt elsewhere, but the original site becomes a Defiled Ruin. Humans with at least 1 sin can burn 100 gc worth of incense in a secret ritual at this site to heal 1d6 wounds.

## 3. Temple of None

- The stairs rise into a long, obsidian room. Against the western wall stands a crumbling dais flanked by rows of carved marble columns.

- A 9-foot-tall statue sits upon a marble throne set on the dais. It would be taller, but its head is missing.

- A golden inscription at the foot of the dais is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

- Behind the dais, an oval of obsidian frames a blank section of wall.

### Demons

Two **demon D** flank the frame behind the dais. They wait patiently unless attacked or otherwise indicated.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech at the foot of the dais reads, “I have no mouth and speak for no one. Listen if you wish to hear nothing.”

### Statue

If PCs approach, they each hear the statue speak in their own voice, which emanates from where its head would be.

It is obviously a magical automaton that cannot think for itself, and its responses quickly become repetitive. It can speak on the following topics.

- **Godswound:** “This scar marks the place where no god was felled. No one weeps for the forgotten.”

- **Threats in the Godswound:** The two demons in this area now attack. The statue shouts over the sounds of battle. “Demons. Shadows of their former selves. But they are nothing compared to the monsters that leave this place.” The latter refers to the PCs, who will leave likely burdened with sin and blasphemies, though the statue will not explain.

- **Treasures in the Godswound:** If the PCs have found the blasphemy of justice in area 2, “That bauble is but a taste of what awaits. Three more there are to open the door to the one who weeps.” If they do not have it, the statue tells them they forgot something in the “circle of lies.”

### Portal

Only once the statue has explained the blasphemies does the frame on the wall blink to reveal an opaque, silvery portal of swirling mist. A creature who enters must make the following test, gaining a +1 bonus for each sin they have.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They teleport to “Betrayal” (area 1). | They teleport to their choice of “Sorrow” (area 4) or “Lies” (area 7). | As tier 2 or “Escape” (area 11). |

## 4. Bitter Sea

- Stinging rain pelts a cold, black, boundless ocean.

- The only illumination comes from far-off lightning bolts and a single, crimson light to the southeast, bobbing atop the roiling waves.

Creatures who are teleported into this room find themselves submerged 5 feet below the surface.

### Ocean

The water is infinitely deep. At the beginning of each round, all creatures in the water are pulled 1d6 squares east toward area 5, which is 20 squares to the south. Creatures without a swim speed who attempt to swim through the rough waters in any direction must make the following test.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They sink 5 feet deeper into the water. | They swim up to half their speed. | They swim up to their speed. |

### Storm

The clouds are infinitely high. Creatures who attempt to fly through the area above the sea must make the following test.

**2d10 + A or S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They are struck by a lightning bolt, take 6d6 P dam and fall. | They fly half their speed, but the storm deals 3d6 P dam. | They fly their speed. |

### Demons

If a PC flails about in the ocean for 2 combat rounds, a **demon G** with a swim speed of 5 emerges from the deep to devour any creatures in the water. The demon can stab spots in the dark water to summon **demon D** with a swim of 4. They pursue intruders to areas 5 and 6 if necessary.

If demon G is killed, their corpse begins to sink 60 feet per round. The corpse can be sliced open to reveal a **masterwork coin purse** containing four rubies (**gems worth 2,700 gc each**).

## 5. Maelstrom of Sorrow

- A massive vortex of water spins to form an enormous vertical funnel.

- Flotsam and other debris collide with devastating impact.

### Vortex

Creatures who attempt to swim or fly in this area use the tests in area 4. Even creatures with a swim speed must make tests to swim, though they gain an edge.

At the beginning of each round, all creatures in the area are slid 2d6 squares counter-clockwise, maintaining the same distance from the center of the vortex. Then they are vertical pulled 1d6 squares toward the center and surface of the water. They take 1 damage for every square they move.

Once they reach the center, they are submerged and take 5d6 damage at the start of each round.

A creature who uses an action to scoop treasure gathers **100 gc** worth of coins for each square they move through the water that round.

## 6. Lonely Buoy

- A steel buoy floats 50 feet east of where creatures teleport into area 4. The buoy is 10 feet wide and slick with rain. A bloody glow shines from its apex 20 feet up.

- A golden inscription on the steel girders is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

### Environs

At the beginning of each round, all creatures in the water are pulled 1d6 squares southwest toward area 5. Creatures who attempt to swim or fly use the tests in area 4.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech on the girders reads, “Save yourself.”

### Buoy

The buoy can hold a maximum of three Medium creatures. If this limit is exceeded, the buoy capsizes, dumping everyone into the ocean before righting itself. A creature who attempts to climb to the beacon must make the following test, taking a bane due to the weather. Creatures with a climb speed take no bane.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The buoy capsizes, dumping everyone on it into the ocean. | They climb halfway. A second test with this result becomes a tier 3. | They reach the beacon. |

### Demons

The first time a creature clambers onto the buoy, **demon E** with a swim speed of 7 uncoils their snakelike body from beneath the beacon to attack.

### Portal

The beacon is the *blasphemy of vitality* (see area 2), a reddish-brown statuette of a human heart carved from yew heartwood (**Tiny art object worth 12,000 gc**). Touching it opens a portal in midair, floating 10 feet west of the buoy’s apex. To enter, a creature must find a way to cross the gap. A creature who enters the portal must make the following test, gaining a +1 bonus for each sin they have.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They teleport to “Sorrow” (area 4). | They teleport to their choice of “Betrayal” (area 1) or “Lies” (area 7). | As tier 2 or “Escape” (area 11). |

### Blasphemy of Vitality

A human who swallows the *blasphemy of vitality* and chooses to use it can never again regain Stamina from a benefaction spell. Instead, they can use a maneuver to wound a human within 50 feet they can see, dealing P dam equal to their Mind and regaining the same amount of Stamina. They also gain AD equal to their Mind, which disappears when they finish a rest.

## 7. Hall of Mirrors

- This is a maze of polished bronze where sputtering torches provide dim light.

- Giggling, unhinged reflections of the PCs mock them from the walls.

- A golden inscription etched in the ground is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech etched in the ground reads, “We plead, gods laugh.”

### Demons

When a creature enters this area, their shadow animates as a **demon B** that attacks the PC, but won’t leave the maze. If a PC takes damage, their shadow demon howls with laughter, and the PC must make the following **Mind RR**.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The PC collapses with laughter, falling prone. They are vulnerable, and all their allies within 30 feet must make this test. | They laugh until the end of their next turn, during which time they are vulnerable. | They maintain control and do not laugh. |

If the PCs have not yet encountered the Laugh Eater, the creature arrives 10 rounds after the first PC laughs, wherever they are. Each time a PC laughs, reduce the remaining rounds by one.

### Blasphemy of Community

The reflection of one PC holds a small, knotted cord of raw platinum-white silk. The reflection will agree to exchange it for treasure worth at least 500 gc. The knot is the *blasphemy of community* (**Tiny art object worth 12,000 gc**).

A human who swallows the *blasphemy of community* and chooses to use it can share their sins with the innocent. As an action, they can transfer some or all of their sin to another human they can see who has no sin. This renders the blasphemy inert.

## 8. Double Bind

- The walls of the bronze maze funnel south, into a long, narrow hallway.

- At the far end stands a plinth with a small, smooth ball of electrum sitting atop.

- A golden inscription behind the plinth is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech behind the plinth reads, “Truth is a prison.”

### Plinth

When a creature picks up the electrum sphere (**Tiny art object worth 1,500 gc**), they can tell instantly this is not a blasphemy. The other end of the hallway slams closed, trapping anyone inside.

### Demons

When the wall shuts, eight **demon A** and one **demon F** rise from the floor to attack. The wall opens when either the demon F or a PC dies.

## 9. Face to Face

- Tiny flecks of darkness float in the air.

- The walls of the bronze maze funnel east into a large room. All the walls are mirrored, making hundreds of repeated reflections.

- A golden inscription etched on the ground is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

- A portal glows around a corner.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech etched on the ground reads, “Every friendship ends with a funeral. Don’t hesitate to make the most of things.””

### Demons

A PC who examines the reflections notices that one has a iridescent eye. A PC can reach into the mirror and pluck the eye from reflection if they act quickly. If they hesitate, that relfection and another step out. The one with the shining eye is a **demon H** and the other is a **clone**. Both have taken the form of the PC with the most TXP. If a PC scores a critical hit against the demon or makes a weapon attack with a double edge against them and gets a tier 3 result, they can choose to remove the eye with the attack.

### Blasphemy of Survival

Demon H’s eye is an enormous pearl with an iris of nacre, the *blasphemy of survival* (**Tiny art object worth 12,000 gc**).

A human who swallows the *blasphemy of survival* and chooses to use it ensures their spirit lives on after death.

If the PC dies and their player makes a new PC to take their place in the same campaign, the new PC is possessed by the dead PC and controls them permanently. The new character has the same memories and knowledge as the dead PC, but not the same XP total. This renders the blasphemy inert.

### Portal

A creature who enters the portal must make the following test, gaining a +1 bonus for each sin they have.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They teleport to “Sorrow” (area 4). | They teleport to their choice of “Betrayal” (area 1) or “Lies” (area 7). | As tier 2 or “Escape” (area 11). |

## 10. House of Regret

- A large table is prepared with hot tea and tea cakes, enough for everyone present stands at the center of the room.

- The food smells inviting, but the decor is threadbare.

- A long window overlooks a city of old, from before the Necromancer Wars.

- The sky is overcast, and fat raindrops pour from the clouds. The room is drafty.

- Seated at the table is a well-dressed human with a sad, familiar face.

- The door and window are locked and unbreakable. There is no way out of the room.

### Reunion

Each PC views the seated human as someone dear to them who died a tragic death. If a PC does not have someone who fits that description, the Ref should work with the player to create one now. The new NPC becomes a permanent addition to the PC’s backstory, and the PC cannot remember a time when this person did not exist. It has always been this way. (This is not just a retcon via game mechanics. An elemental is rewriting history.)

“I never thought I’d see you again,” the loved one says, using any terms of endearment that would be appropriate. “Welcome to the house of regret. Sit with me a while.” They turn to stare wistfully out the window. They can speak on anything they knew in life, as well as the following topics.

- **Is it Really You?** “In a sense. I feel as if I’m really here. There is a presence that animates me. The elemental, I think.”

- **Life After Death:** “It’s hazy. You’ll have to wait and find out for yourself.”

- **The Elemental:** “A creation of magic itself, born of the world’s own trauma. They plucked me from your past so we could speak, but they do not view things the way we do. The tragedy of my death helps them bring me into focus.”

- **The Godswound:** “Before the Necromancer Wars, there were six gods. One of them, now forgotten, intervened, while the five everyone knows about abstained. No one knows whether this sixth god opposed the necromancers, supported them, chose sides, or perhaps even prolonged the war. But meddling so directly in human affairs was enough for the other gods to join together to destroy them. But a god cannot die. It’s anathema to reality, so reality rewrote itself. There have always been only five gods, and they are not murderers. Indeed, they don’t even remember the act. Only here, in this crevice outside of reality, is the truth still known. And now you know it, too. The strain of this feat twisted magic itself into a cruel and fickle force. Priests and followers of the forgotten god don’t realize the fellowship that was stripped from them. They feel something is wrong with the world but cannot pinpoint what.”

- **Denizens of Godswound: “**Hell, filled with sinners against a god that never was, disgorged these poor souls. But with no one to claim them, they are half-damned, their tainted spirits cursed to roam the world as creatures of shadow. The sixth god’s celestial servants were cast down, too, lingering as mere wisps of the starry heavens, ever wailing at a loss they cannot recall.”

### Rededication

Once the PCs have heard the true story of Godswound, the loved one asks, “I have told you what there is to know. Do you wish to cast off who you are and remember who you never were?” There is no coercion. The loved one has no stake in this decision.

Any PC who agrees suddenly feels like their heart is being ripped from their chest, but they remain unharmed. They gain an additional background, the Heretic Background. This special background adds additional features to the one they rolled at character creation. The new equipment materializes out of the shadows.

If the PC who agrees has one of the Acolyte backgrounds, they instead gain the Apostate

Background.

Once every PC has decided one way or another, the loved one stands. “I wish we had more time. But I have to go. And you have one more choice to make.”

Where once there was a single door, now there are two. Or maybe there always were two. “To the left, peace and safety. To the right, a change, in you or your fate.” The left door teleports creatures to area 11. The right door teleports them to area 12. All four blasphemies are required to open it.

### Heretic Background

You have never truly venerated the traitor gods.

**Sin:** 1 sin.

**Characteristic:** Increase Mind by 1. If Mind is already 4, increase Stamina by 2 instead.

**Trait:** Necromancy: Demon’s Sight. You gain this trait even if you do not meet the prerequisites, and the effect is always active.

**Expertises:** You gain Endurance, Religious Lore, and Stealth.

**Equipment:** knife, spellbook: *corrupt*

### Apostate Background

Your loyalty to the traitor gods has curdled to loathing.

**Sin:** Gain 1 sin.

**Characteristic:** Increase Mind by 1. If Mind is already 4, increase Stamina by 2 instead.

**Traits:** Knowledge: Proselytize (functions as Knowledge: Shared Knowledge), Necromancy: Demon’s Sight (always active). You gain these traits even if you do not meet the prerequisites.

**Expertises:** You gain Historical Lore, Magic Lore, Necromancy, and Religious Lore.

**Equipment:** masterwork lore book (Religious Lore), spellbook: *to hell*

## 11. Tollbooth

- A cobblestone plaza hangs suspended in a dim void. The closed iris of a portal lies horizontally at the center.

- A sandstone altar, stained with blood, stands nearby. A book lies in the middle.

- Beyond the edge of the platform, a sharp drop falls away to nothingness.

- A golden inscription around the portal is written in a language that hurts the eyes.

### Inscription

When translated, the demonic speech around the portal reads, “The dead cost nothing.”

### Altar

The book is a **summon hireling spellbook**. The portal only opens when 5,000 gc or more worth of treasure is placed on the altar, which then disappears. Alternatively, a human can be sacrificed upon it. The portal leads to the top of the cliffs surrounding Godswound. Once used, it closes again. Each time it is reopened, the treasure cost increases by 5,000 gc.

### Ledge

Anything that falls off the edge plummets forever. Creatures eventually die of exposure.

## 12. Ampule of Grief

- You stand on the floor in the center of an enormous room made entirely of transparent crystal that holds an inky void at bay.

- Enormous growths of crystal dot the floor.

- Floating overhead, a 100-foot-tall constellation of glass teardrops slowly twists and whirls. Beads within continually shatter, each prismatic pop of light releasing a wracked sob.

### Elemental of Regret

Every creature present understands. This is a battle for the right to change their own fate.

The Elemental of Regret opens battle with Rain of Sorrow, Strain Brain, and Warp World, attempting to affect as many PCs as possible. They then use their attacks to deal damage to the most vulnerable PCs before repeating the process on those who dared to resist their earlier efforts.

### Rewards

Any PC who survives once the elemental is destroyed is teleported to the top of the cliffs surrounding the Godswound. The PCs receive **2d6 elemental parts** and a pile of **30,000 gc**. They must each choose their own reward from the following options.

- Revive one creature that died in the fight with the elemental. They appear here unharmed.

- Swap two characteristics scores.

- Swap a trait for its XP cost, which can be spent immediately.

- Lose all uses of one effort and reapportion them as you desire.

- Save a mote of fate for the future. One time, after you or a creature within 10 squares of you makes a test, you can set the result to your choice of crit or doom.
