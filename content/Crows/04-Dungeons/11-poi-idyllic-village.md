# POI: Idyllic Village

> Source: Crows — Dungeons, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 4–5. Original playtest wording retained.

When the PCs reach the village, they find:

- An idyllic village rests between rolling hills, the smell of freshly baked bread wafting on a pleasant breeze as laughter and music ring from village square.

- Listless villagers lounge around a figure singing in the middle of the square. Their song sinks into your senses, rich and inviting in a way you’ve never heard before.

- A trail of food and treasure leads from their pavilion to the town hall where it spills out of its open doors like a cornucopia.

This village is held captive by an **angel D** who warps the minds of those who wander close. Prolonged practice of their song has allowed them to control the minds of the villagers much longer than usual.

Instead of a deteriorating village, those who hear their song see a picturesque, safe village led by someone with unearthly beauty. The townspeople are charmed into serving them, and the angel and their kin collect their offerings, then feed on the villagers when they hunger.

A PC who approaches the village must make the following test.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The PC is overcome with the urge to rest, abandon their worries, and offer their belongings to this figure of beauty and simply be ... at any cost. | The PC is lulled into contentment and desires to listen and appreciate the beauty here. Nothing short of a bribe worth at least 100 gc or the threat of violence can convince them otherwise. | The PC resists the mind control and sees the dilapidated village and angel for what it is. |

If a PC gets a tier 1 or tier 2 result, the angel’s mind control has begun to take hold. It must be interrupted by some means, such as moving a safe distance away from the village or blocking their hearing. A PC who got a tier 2 result can take actions to interrupt their own mind control, but they take a bane on any tests required to do so. A PC who got a tier 1 result can’t help themself—they don’t want to. If a PC resists the mind control or breaks it, they don’t need to make a test against it again.

## Village Square

- Light seems to adorn the singing figure like a mantle.

- Scavenged food and recovered art are gathered behind them in the town hall, piled high.

- With dull eyes and adoring smiles, villagers bring offerings to the pavilion, adding them to the pile. Their frames are gaunt despite the gathered food.

- A few villagers are well-kept, nourished, and gorgeous to behold. They stand away from the pavilion, watching the proceedings with keen interest.

### Angels

The **angel D** singing controls the villagers’ minds, compelling them to give food and offerings as tribute. Four **angel C** who live in this village benefit from this extortion, though the angel D demands the lion’s share.

The angel C keep their distance from the villagers, as humans who come within 5 feet of them and see their true appearance tend to snap out of their mind control long enough to create a scene.

The angels are hostile to anyone who tries to interrupt their plans or who they catch stealing from their hoard. Otherwise, they’re content to live in indulgence with access to easy prey.

### Villagers

There are approximately fifty villagers. Most of them are former merchants or crows who were drawn into this trap when they approached the village to scavenge for loot.

If the angels are defeated, the villagers can see the dilapidated village for what it is: a derelict settlement at the mercy of the Miasma and likely beyond saving.

## Town Hall

- Stairs lead up to a two-story town hall with large arch doorways set in a timber frame structure and adorned with steep gable roofs.

- Baskets and satchels of food and goods are stacked high within, pouring out of the front in glorious excess.

- Everything is made desperately beautiful— arranged in withering leaves and woven baskets, adorned with torn fabric to mimic ribbons, each gift more lavish than the last.

### Offerings

Many of the food offerings are made to look edible by the angel’s controlled perception of the village. If the angels are defeated and the mind control is dispelled, most of the food offerings are revealed to be rotting. However, there is enough food to make up **16 rations**.

Alongside the food are many beautiful treasures, including six family heirloom necklaces (**Tiny art objects worth 50 gc each**), four wooden antique statues (**Small art objects worth 20 gc each**), two tapestries (**Medium art objects worth 100 gc each**), a dozen metal platters and serving dishes (**Small art objects worth 10 gc each**), and a gilded statue made to look like the angel’s illusory form (**Large art object worth 1,000 gc**).

The villagers are willing to part with these goods in exchange for freeing them.
