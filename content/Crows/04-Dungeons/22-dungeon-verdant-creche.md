# Dungeon: Verdant Creche

> Source: Crows — Dungeons, Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 46–55. Original playtest wording retained.

*This dungeon is a fun challenge with a chance of death for three to five 4th-6th level characters.*

When the necromancer Amaryll realized their plant monster creations were better in every way than humans, they set out to give them an idyllic home, a place of rest and nourishment, safe from the coming strife. And what better place for Amaryll to continue their research?

The Necromancer Wars demanded more of Amaryll’s attention than they had hoped. But the sanctuary they created and its inhabitants thrived in their absence, certainly more than the servants of those other power hungry magicians. The plant creatures here have been primarily selfsufficient. Only recently have there been tales of green-leafed horrors raiding the countryside for meat.

The Creche is filled with deadly creatures and dangerous challenges. Only experienced crows stand a chance of surviving its horrors and claiming the riches within.

**What the PCs don’t know** is that Amaryll’s research into the natural regenerative properties of plants led to their greatest and most controversial experiment. And it is about to bloom.

## Creche Approach

When the PCs reach the creche, they notice the following details:

- An enormous, domed greenhouse composed of intersecting triangular glass panes sits atop a low, grass-covered hill. Glass double doors at ground level face east.

- The thick glass panes are warm to the touch and fogged over from within.

- Creeping ivy covers much of the outside of the dome, making it easy to climb. The apex of the dome is 100 feet above the ground.

- The entire dome is filled with dense foliage, swaying in some unseen breeze.

### Outside the Creche

A PC who spends an hour searching the hill finds **1d6 rations** or **animal feed** (player’s choice) worth of nutritious vegetables growing wild in the lush soil. After 20 units of food have been harvested, the land falls barren, but magically replenishes itself by the next morning.

Aside from the greenhouse, the grassy hill is otherwise bare. Any PC that touches the ground within 50 feet of the greenhouse is dusted with a cloud of spores. The spores lie dormant until the PC returns to their village, where they germinate and begin to grow into plant monsters. At the end of that village cycle, those monsters attack the village (as the -6 entry on the Village Events table in Characters) before vanishing into the wilderness.

PCs can disinfect themselves by using soap to thoroughly wash away the spores from their bodies and equipment before returning to their village. A PC can make the following test to see what they know about the spores.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They know the spores are harmless to humans. | They know the spores are harmless to humans, but plants can detect them. | As tier 2, and they know the spores will sprout into plant monsters within a fortnight. |

## Entering the Creche

The PCs can enter the creche in any of the following ways:

- Through the double doors, which are locked. A PC can attempt to pick the lock with the following test. If the doors remain intact, they relock once closed.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One of the doors shatters loudly, triggering an encounter check Creatures in area 1 are on alert and begin combat hidden. | The doors unlock with a loud clang. Creatures in area 1 are on alert and begin combat hidden. | The doors unlock. |

- They can break a glass pane. The dome is constructed from 1,000 triangular panes of magically reinforced glass, each roughly 10 feet across. Each pane has 20 Stamina. When a pane is destroyed, it shatters loudly, triggering an encounter check. If a pane ever breaks while the PCs are engaged in combat, increase the EN by 1 when making the encounter check. Plant creatures pursue any escaping PCs they can see as far as the base of the hill.

- If more than half the dome’s panes are destroyed, the entire structure collapses, and all creatures and objects beneath it take 30 damage. If any plant creatures survive, they rebuild the dome in 1d6 months.

When the PCs enter the creche, the first Dungeon Turn begins. Let them know, and start the 30-minute timer.

### Shared Details

Unless otherwise noted, all interior areas of the creche share the following details:

- **Flora, Not Fauna:** Every surface and patch of ground is covered in plants and fungi, both natural and unnatural. As in a typical rain forest, insects, birds, fish, small mammals, and lizards abound. But upon close inspection all the animals are actually plant creatures that mimic animal shapes and behavior. If a stat block is needed, choose a Tiny or Small animal from The Ref and change its type to Plant. This also makes it ineligible as a pet.

- **Open Concept:** Encounter areas are separated by natural terrain features, not walls and doors. Trees and rocks 10 feet or larger in diameter provide cover. Combat that lasts longer than three consecutive rounds draws the attention of creatures in an adjacent area and also triggers an encounter check.

- **Map:** Areas of the greenhouse are keyed to the Verdant Creche map.

Only areas of the creche **above ground level (areas 1 through 7)** share the following details:

- **Extreme Humidity:** A wet haze shrouds everything. Non-plant creatures treat everything more than one square away from them as having light concealment. The haze imposes no penalties for plant creatures.

- **Oppressive Heat:** The glass panes amplify light and heat. At the end of each DT, PCs must make a **Strength RR**.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2d6 P dam | 1d6 P dam | No effect |

- **Ceiling Height:** The dome is a half-sphere 200 feet across at ground level. The height of the ceiling above a spot on the ground is approximately the same as the horizontal distance to the closest part of the dome. So 60 feet from the dome, the ceiling is 60 feet high. Tell Euclid to stuff it.

- **Hedge Ring:** A 30-foot high wall of thorny hedges encircles the arboretum (area 7) acts as an alarm. Any attempt to bypass the hedges or climb or fly over them alerts the creatures in areas 6 and 7.

Only areas of the creche **below ground level (areas 8 through 13)** share the following details:

- **Darkness:** The caves have no light sources.

- **Ceiling Height:** Ceilings in the caves are 30 feet high.

- **Slick Walls:** The bedrock walls are slippery with rot and slime. Creatures who attempt to climb the walls must make the following test, gaining an edge if they have a climb speed.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They fall. | They climb up to twice their speed, but the stress of the climb deals 1 P dam. | They climb up to twice their speed. |

### Sneaking, Likes, and Hates

The creche is filled with plant guardians who keep a watchful eye for intruders. If PCs want to avoid combat in this dungeon, the most straightforward approach is to utilize stealth and the likes and hates of plant creatures to cause distractions. Players may also have equipment or creative ideas that allow them to get by the plants without battle. Remember to be open to those!

## Creche Encounters

The EN for the creche is 3. If at least one PC or ally is covered in spores, or Mulch has been harvested, the EN is 4. When a random encounter occurs, roll on the Plant Creature Encounters table in *The Ref*.

In the creche, open areas make it easy for enemies to swarm. In a war of attrition, the plant creatures will win. Crows who survive leverage every advantage they can, like stealth, hit-and-run tactics, ambushes, or immediate and overwhelming violence. A working knowledge of plant creatures’ likes and hates will also come in handy.

## 1. Hanging Garden

- A wide thoroughfare of soft moss and long grasses, flanked on either side by rows of 30-foot conifers.

- Through the trees to the southwest is a wide pond (area 2).

- Through the trees to the northwest are rows of colorful flowers (area 5).

- A ring of hedges at the far end of the thoroughfare blocks access to the center of the dome. Behind the hedges rises a single, enormous tree with spreading branches that brush the top of the dome 100 feet above.

- On each side of the path, human corpses dangle from vines on ivy-wrapped posts, skeletal hands frozen in a claw at their necks. While some of the corpses appear to have been armed, others were obviously noncombatants, not crows.

### Thoroughfare

One **plant A** for each creature in the party lies scattered in the grass of the thoroughfare. If the PCs made too much noise entering the greenhouse, the plant creatures are completely hidden and wait to attack as many creatures as possible simultaneously who enter the thoroughfare.

### Skeletons

In total there are six hanging corpses, three on each side of the thoroughfare. They have been stripped of flesh and equipment made of organic materials, though several still wear metal.

- **Skeleton A:** Missing a leg. A gold signet ring with the monogram “G” is stuck on one fingerbone, which must be removed to retrieve it. The ring is a **Tiny art object worth 100 gc**.

- **Skeleton B:** Still equipped with a cavalry sabre (**steel sword**) and black lacquered lamellar (**archmage obsidian heavy armor**). If this skeleton is disturbed, a **plant E** takes flight from the top of the post and investigates 1d6 combat rounds later.

- **Skeleton C:** Just a skull and spine. The other bones lie in a broken pile at the bottom of the post, along with a **steel axe**.

- **Skeleton D:** Smaller than the other skeletons.

- **Skeleton E:** Nothing of value remains on the corpse, but four gemstones have fallen into thick grass at the bottom of the post: a fire opal (**gem worth 250 gc**), a pearl (**gem worth 150 gc**), an onyx (**gem worth 200 gc**), and a moonstone (**gem worth 100 gc**).

- **Skeleton F:** Skull caved in. Still clutching the remains of a now useless spellbook and an iron **holy symbol** of the Gardner.

## 2. Irrigation Pond

- A shallow, freshwater pond dotted with yardwide lily pads and ringed with green algae.

- Something shiny glints at the bottom of the pond.

- Through the trees to the northeast is an open thoroughfare (area 1).

- Up an embankment to the west the rocky terrain rises into a trio of spires (area 3).

- Hedges block the path to the center of the dome (area 7).

### Rejuvenation Pool

A living creature that drinks a pint of water from the pond recovers as if they had used a **healing potion**. The water can also be stored for later use. After 1d6 pints of water have been removed from the pool, its magic is spent and does not replenish until the next morning. A PC can make the following test to examine the water.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No info | They know the water is potable. | They know the water has healing properties. |

### Bait

At the center of the pond under several feet of water shines a filigreed platinum necklace (**Tiny art object worth 1,000 gc**). The necklace appears to be stuck and cannot be moved with a pole or using magic, because a **plant D** hidden beneath the murky water has it pinned under one of their legs. They attack any human they notice who enters the water, using their Pinning Leg to hold them prone beneath the water until they drown. If the creature uses their acid attack, it affects every non-plant creature in or touching the water.

## 3. Lichen Spires

- Three hollow towers of crumbling stone spotted with lichen stretch high into the air. One is 20 feet tall, one is 40 feet tall, and one is 60 feet tall.

- A grassy meadow lies north (area 4).

- To the east, a pond lies at the foot of the towers (area 2).

- Hedges block the path to the center of the dome (area 7).

### Rocky Butte

This area sits on a slab of bedrock jutting out of the soil. It is difficult terrain.

### Trio of Spires

A PC can make the following test to climb the inside of one of the spires. PCs who have a full backpack take a bane, as do PCs who wear armor, due to the extra weight. PCs who successfully summit a spire gain a double edge to descend that same spire.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The spire collapses on top of them. They take 1d6 P dam for every 10 feet of the spire’s height. This triggers an encounter check. | They climb to their destination but emerge scratched and bruised. They take 1 dam for every 10 feet of the spire’s height. | They climb to their destination. |

If a spire collapses, any treasure inside can be dug out of the rubble with 10 combat rounds of uninterrupted labor. A PC can take damage to reduce the time needed by one round per point of damage, to a minimum of one round.

- **Spire A:** 20 feet high. A creature at the top can see the layout of areas 2 and 4 and the creatures within. Near the summit, a plant hawk nest holds three plant hawk eggs (**worth 200 gc apiece if sold to a farmer, falconer, or chef; use Tiny art objects card**), a **chicken bag**, and a **fine coin purse** that contains **620 gc**.

- **Spire B:** 40 feet high. This spire crumbles easily, so attempts to climb it take a bane. Any creature that tries to climb it can tell it is fragile and abort the attempt before making the test. A creature at the top can see everything they could see from spire A, and they can also see over the hedges into area 7. They can also retrieve a magic **necromancer deathtree longbow with the hungry property** that has been stashed here.

- **Spire C:** 60 feet high. From the top, a creature can see the layout of every area in the greenhouse and the creatures within. The summit is two squares above and three squares southwest of the westernmost branch in area 6. Enterprising PCs could cross between the two points.

## 4. Caretaker’s Meadow

- A grassy meadow ringed by trees.

- A decrepit wooden shack leans against the trunk of one of the trees.

- To the south, three rocky spires rise high into the air (area 3).

- Rows of colorful flowers lie east (area 5).

- The ring of hedges opens to the southeast, allowing access to the giant tree in area 7.

### Shack

The ill-fitting door has no lock, and only a small hole where a window would normally be. One corner of the shack is filled with freshly tilled soil. The inside walls of the hovel act as storage for gardening implements: **2 shovels**, a **knife**, and **3 buckets**. There is a 70% chance the caretaker is working inside the shack. Otherwise, they are tending the flowers in the meadow when the PCs arrive.

### Caretaker

A **plant G** is the tireless caretaker for the creche, using their tendrils to perform tasks that require fine motor skills or a strong arm. They are not in charge of the other plant creatures here and do not (cannot, in fact) control them. Instead, they serve, diligently maintaining Amaryll’s work until the necromancer returns someday. If the PCs appear hostile to any plants or plant creatures, the caretaker attacks.

The caretaker and the creche are inextricably linked. If the caretaker dies, another one is birthed from the soil of the shack 1d6 x 10 minutes later.

## 5. Poppy Field

- Neat rows of colorful, fragrant flowers.

- To the south is an open thoroughfare (area 1).

- A grassy meadow lies east (area 4).

- Hedges block the path to the center of the dome (area 7).

### Poppies

Non-plant creatures in this area must make the following **Strength RR** when they enter it and at the beginning of every combat round.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They fall asleep, unconscious. If they wake, drowsiness leaves them weakened. | Drowsiness leaves them weakened. | No effect. |

### Hidden enemies

Only after at least one PC has been put to sleep do the denizens of the poppy field make themselves known. Four **plant C** crawl out of the hedge ring to attack fallen foes.

## 6. Boughs Above

- The wide branches of the central tree (see area 7) spread high in the air, providing perches and walkways 50 feet and 80 feet above the ground.

- Branches thick with leaves provide cover to all creatures in this area.

A PC who climbs the outside of the dome and breaks a pane near the apex could find a way to lower themself down to this area, entering here instead of area 1.

### Trunk

The thick bark of this enormous tree offers easy handholds for climbers to move between both levels of branches or down to the ground.

### Canopy

A single **plant E** flies lazily through the branches. When it spots prey, it attacks. Four **plant B** cling to the branches on the lower level and fire berry bombs. The westernmost branch 50 feet above the ground is two squares below and three squares northeast of the summit of the tallest spire in area 3. Enterprising PCs could cross between the two points.

### Treasure

A PC who searches the crook of the upper branches for 10 minutes can spot a particularly reactive section of heartwood. Using an axe or similar implement, they can retrieve 1d6 **elemental parts** from that section once.

## 7. Arboretum

- An enormous tree stands at the very center of the greenhouse, its topmost branches brushing the apex of the dome.

- A 30-foot high hedge ring separates this area from all others except areas 4 and 6.

### Central Tree

Creatures can easily climb into the higher branches. The tree has 100 Stamina. If it is destroyed, it topples. Roll 1d6. On a 1-5, the result indicates which area it lands in, destroying all objects and creatures in that area and exposing the underground tunnel. On a 6, the trunk splinters and the tree falls straight down, destroying areas 6 and 7. PCs in the area where the tree falls must make an **Agility RR**.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The PC is reduced to 0 Stamina and falls prone. If their Stamina is already 0, they take 1d6 wounds. | They retreat to an adjacent area (determined at random), but the stress of the escape deals 1d6 P dam. | They retreat to an adjacent area of their choice. |

### Grove Guardians

In the shade of the larger tree stand two **plant F**, gnarled, knotty trees 15 feet tall, their enormity dwarfed by their larger cousin. They attack any humans they notice who enter the hedge ring or approach the larger tree, grappling enemies and throwing them high in the air. They can reach the lower branches of area 6 with their corpse attack. If they become overwhelmed, the creatures from area 6 descend to lend aid.

### Roots

A 10-foot wide tunnel is hidden beneath one of the huge roots of the central tree. A PC can make the following test to discern what the trees are protecting.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No info | They notice the opening of the tunnel, but the grove guardians move to intercept. | They notice the opening of the tunnel, which is unguarded for the moment. |

## 8. Tunnel

Darkness presses in, and the heat of the greenhouse fades. It is hard to hold onto the slick walls of this nearly vertical tunnel (see Shared Details, earlier). Anyone who falls slides down the chute and lands in the middle of area 9.

## 9. Reclamation Room

- The tunnel exits from the rocky cave ceiling. It is a sheer drop 30 feet down into a 100-footwide pit filled with refuse, rotting plant creatures, half-eaten human and animal corpses and other organic detritus.

- A 5-foot-wide ledge runs around the entire perimeter of the pit, just above the surface of the detritus. The ledge connects to a tunnel that exits to the east, and another tunnel that exits to the west.

- A rumbling, churning sound echoes through the cavern, and a wave of detritus is thrown in the air as something moves quickly beneath it.

### Detritus Pit

The detritus is 20 feet deep. The muck is soft, and creatures who fall into it take no falling damage. PCs who cannot keep their heads above the surface risk suffocating. The refuse is so thick, swimming or burrowing are the only ways to move through it. PCs who make the attempt must use the following test, gaining an edge if they have a swim or burrow speed.

**2d10 + S**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They make no progress and sink 1 square deeper into the detritus. | They move up to twice their speed, but the stress deals 1 P dam. | They move up to twice their speed. |

### Trash Collector

If more than one non-plant creature enters this room at a time, the whole area rumbles loudly. If more than one creature remains in this area for more than 2 combat rounds, a **plant H** breaches the surface of the muck. They are large enough to walk on the bottom of the pit and move unhindered with their burrow speed.

They attempt to devour any creatures stuck in the muck before turning their attention to creatures on the ledge and walls. Any attack the behemoth misses against a creature on the ledge destroys an adjacent 10-foot section of ledge. They do not leave this area.

### Leftovers

PCs who search the detritus might retrieve something valuable. They might also suffer from exposure to the poisonous filth. For every 5 minutes outside of combat a PC spends sifting through, dredging, or fishing in the muck of the pit, they find 2d10 **plant parts** and must make a **Strength RR** against toxins. Sifting through the garbage for 5 minutes also alerts the plant H here (see Trash Collector).

**2d10 + S**

| --- | --- | --- |
| 1d6 P dam and weakened | 1d6 P dam | No effect |

- A cacophony of smells assails the nostrils. Individually they are pleasant, but together, they are confusing.

## 11. Graft Fair

- This long, narrow cavern echoes with what sounds like marching feet. It curves southwest and empties into a winding tunnel.

- Along the inner wall, dozens of human and animal limbs dangle from butcher’s hooks. (*If a PC lost a limb (or head) to one of the plant E attacks in the greenhouse earlier, it is here hanging on a hook.*)

### Dress Up

An arachnid-shaped **plant D** props themself up on four legs in a corner. Their other four legs have been removed, replaced with four new mammalian limbs, all seemingly from different creatures. They seem to be practicing to walk with their new legs.

Three **plant B** gingerly steady severed heads with their tendrils, balancing them like hats atop their leafy crowns.

Though they have no faces, the plant creatures seem almost embarrassed for a moment if they notice the PCs. Then they attack with fury. The plant D uses one arachnid leg to wield an axe, adding the Dismember trait to their leg attack.

### Treasure

A woven reed basket in the corner of the room holds a horrific assortment of bloodstained jewelry that humans typically wear on hands or feet: anklets, rings, bracelets, cuffs, and the like. Altogether, there are 30 pieces of jewelry (**Tiny art objects each worth 80 gc**).

## 12. Winding Tunnel

Areas 10 and 11 meet here. The slope of the walls seems to absorb the noise of the other rooms. Here it is peaceful and serene. To the south, a stone hallway opens into a large, long room.

## 13. Nursery

- Withered vines cover the ground, but a few visible patches of dirt appear freshly tilled.

- Two rows of leafy vegetables are planted along the western wall. Once well-tended, the beds are now overgrown, and most of the plants have rotted in the ground.

- A moldering writing desk stands in the northeast corner. A rusted key sits in the lock.

### Buried Ambush

A **plant G** lies in wait beneath the fresh dirt. They burst out of the ground by the cabbage patch if the desk trap is triggered, the journal is removed, or if anyone tries to touch or harm the living plant in the cabbage patch. Ten **plant A** also burrow themselves into position, ready to block an exit from the room. The plant creatures here have been tasked with guarding Amaryll’s experiment and will fight to the death. They will not attack or harm Mulch for any reason, but creatures beyond this area do not have the same limitation.

### Amaryll’s Desk

The key snaps off in the lock if it is used, but the desk is so rotten anyone can pry the mechanism out of the front panel with a knife or strong fingers. The desk unfolds to reveal a researcher’s workspace.

A drawer contains three intact volumes: a **masterwork lore book (Nature Lore)**, a **fine lore book (Alchemy)**, and a **renew spellbook**. A wooden stand holds four glass vials: **two greater healing potions**, a **rage potion**, and a **speed potion**.

A **quill & inkpot** and **masterwork alchemist’s tools** sit atop a moldy journal. The inkpot is trapped, set to trigger if either the inkpot or the journal move. When triggered, it explodes, destroying any of the glass vials that remain in the stand and soaking the journal with ink. Attempts to decipher the journal afterward take a double bane.

A PC who spends 5 minutes examining the workspace spots a tripwire fashioned from a strand of cornsilk. A PC can make the following test to disarm it.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The trap is triggered. | The PC can safely remove the journal and vials, but the trap remains armed. | The trap is disarmed. |

Non-plant creatures within 3 squares of the inkpot when it explodes must make the following **Agility RR**.

**2d10 + A**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2d6 P dam and vulnerable | Vulnerable | No effect |

### Amaryll’s Journal

This simple leather notebook is filled with diagrams and notes in a spidery script, though most of the information is lost to decay. A PC can make the following test to decipher Amaryll’s writings.

**2d10 + M**

| ≤11 | 12-16 | 17+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They know the info from the first sentence of Mulch. | They know the info from the first three paragraphs of Mulch. | As tier 2, and they learn the secret info from the last two paragraphs of Mulch. |

### Cabbage Patch

Two dozen cabbage-like plants lie rotting and split open on the ground. Only one leafy bundle still thrives, ringed by a patch of dirt where no other plants seem to grow. If any creature approaches the ring, the **plant G** beneath the dirt bursts forth to attack.

A PC who examines the last plant finds Mulch, an unfamiliar plant creature nestled in the leaves. They appear much like a newborn human, but their skin is greenish and woody, and they have leaves instead of hair. The creature’s loud cries can only be silenced with food. Any organic material will do, but raw meat and bones sates them for longer.

Once Mulch is harvested from the patch, the ground lies fallow forever. There is only one Mulch.

### Mulch

Amaryll’s journal refers fondly to the plant baby as Mulch. Their purpose is to extend human life at the cost of their own, using the regenerative properties and hardy physiology of plants. Mulch can consume a single human corpse and reinvigorate the body. Mulch then dies, and the corpse returns to life with the physical body of a young adult, with full Stamina and healed of any conditions or diseases.

Mulch ages like a human, only much faster. If fed and watered, they reach the size, appearance, and capabilities of a 10-year-old child within an hour of being harvested. Thereafter, they age one year each day, dying of old age after about three months.

Once able to walk, Mulch acts as a loyal hireling (**Commoner**) to whichever PC took care of them as an infant. Mulch does not need to be paid, but eats twice as much food as a human.

Mulch is not picky about their diet. If left unattended, they will eat any corpse, of any variety, though only a human corpse can be revived. This means if not kept under a watchful eye, Mulch may inadvertently revive a recently slain enemy, a rival, or a body from the village crypt. But perhaps that is what the PCs want to happen.

PCs who achieve a tier 3 result to decipher Amaryll’s journal learn the full truth about the revivification process. The necromancer sought to replace human life, not extend it. In reality, Mulch consumes both the body and soul of the corpse, then transforms into a plant/human hybrid that has the appearance, memories and personality of the deceased. This new being remains a plant creature, can gain nourishment from any organic material, and ages at half the rate of a normal human.

In the journal, Amaryll speculates whether the hybrid is actually the original person grown anew, some kind of merging between Mulch and the deceased, or just an abominable approximation that believes they are someone they are not. The hybrids usually have their own strong feelings about what they have become.
