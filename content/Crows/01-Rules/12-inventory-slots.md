# Inventory Slots

> Source: Crows — Rules Booklet for Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 11–12.

The number of items you can carry is represented by your inventory slots. Every PC has 2 hand slots, 4 belt slots, 3 magic item slots, and 10 backpack slots, numbered 1 to 10. A PC can wear an outfit of clothes (but not armor) and a backpack without those items taking up slots.

Many items, such as a shield, a sword, or a crowbar, take up a single inventory slot. Other items, such as a ladder or a suit of heavy armor, take up more than 1 inventory slot. The inventory slots these items take up must always be of the same type and must be next to each other. You can’t carry a 10-foot pole with one hand slot and one belt slot, and you can’t place your 10-foot pole in backpack slots 2 and 7.

There are several items that can be stacked in a single inventory slot. An inventory slot can hold 5 potions, 3 locks, or 2 flasks of lantern oil. You can only stack items of the same kind in one inventory slot, though these items don’t have to be exactly the same. You can stack up to 5 different potions together in a slot, but you can’t stack 3 potions and 2 locks together. Each hand can only hold one item at a time, so you can’t stack items in hand slots.

## Magic Item Slots

You have 3 magic item slots, which can be occupied by magic items that are classified as “wearable” on their item cards. These items include magic belts, hats, rings, and the like. It doesn’t include magic armor, which is worn the same as mundane armor (see Wearing Armor).

You can wear more than 3 magic items in these slots, but if you do your body is overwhelmed with chaos, you can’t rest, and at the end of each DT, you gain 1d6 wounds.

Use common sense when determining if you can wear certain items with others. For instance, you can’t wear two pairs of magic shoes (unless you grow a second set of feet), but you could wear three magic rings on one hand.

## Item Cards

You track of your equipment using the item cards and inventory sheet that come with this game. You can use the digital files of this game to print as many cards and sheets as you need.

Each piece of equipment is represented by a card. If a piece of equipment takes up 2 slots, you can lay it horizontally to indicate the number of slots it takes up. If it takes up 1 slot, then place it vertically on the sheet. You can keep track of magic effects or the number of an item that you have in a stack by putting the card in a plastic card sleeve and writing on the card in dry erase marker, or write directly on a card you print out.

The cards can be placed on the sheet, or you can arrange them without a sheet by placing your item cards in four rows: the top row contains the cards in your hand slots, the middle row is your belt slot cards, and the bottom two rows are your backpack slot cards (5 slots per backpack row).

## Wearing Armor

You can choose one suit of armor (not a shield) in your backpack slots to be the armor you currently wear. You can only don a new armor when you are not in a situation tracked in combat rounds.

## Equipped Items

Aside from magic items worn in slots and suits of armor, to use any other item, including a weapon, tool, light source, or spellbook, the item must first be placed in a hand slot. Items in hand slots are equipped and being wielded.

## Swapping Slots

When you aren’t in combat or some other situation tracked in combat rounds, you can freely switch items between slots. When you are in a situation tracked in combat rounds, you need to use a maneuver (see Combat) to rearrange your hand slots and belt slots with each other. As part of this maneuver, you can freely drop any item you’re holding or wearing on your belt.

If you want to grab an item from your backpack when time is tracked in combat rounds, you need to use a maneuver and then declare the item you want from your backpack. Then roll 1d10. If the result is equal to or higher than at least one of the backpack slots of the item you declared, you draw out the item and can swap it and any items in your hand slots, or drop the items in your hand slots and move the item from your backpack slots into your hand slots. If the number you rolled is lower, you can rearrange the items in your backpack, but you can’t take anything out. You can only grab one backpack item at a time.

You can drop any number of items from your backpack onto the ground as a maneuver.

## Carrying Corpses

A corpse takes up a number of slots equal to its size (as shown on the Corpse Slots table), plus the number of slots occupied by any equipment on the corpse.

**Corpse Slots**

| Corpse Size | Slots Occupied | Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny | 1 | 3 |
| Small | 2 | 1 |
| Medium | 4 | 1 |
| Large | 8 | 1 |
| Huge | 16 | 1 |
| Holy Shit! | 32 | 1 |

*At the Ref’s discretion, Holy Shit! corpses take up more slots.
