# Overland Travel

> Source: Crows — Rules Booklet for Playtest 3, PDF page(s) 24–29.

Overland travel occurs when the PCs move from one location to another across vast swaths of land or explore to discover points of interest.

## Hexes

Overland travel takes place in increments of hexes placed of a map. Each hex is 5 miles across. Each hex has a habitat type, such as forest, mountain, or plains, that can affect the types of encounters that can occur in the hex.

## Travel Procedure

Each day that the group travels, the Ref should resolve overland travel in the following order.

1. The PCs set their travel pace and route.

2. Each PC and hireling declares a travel role and make any tests related to the role if they undertake a risky task. Supporters make their tests first, then guides, then scouts, and finally trackers.

3. The Ref determines how far the group will get and makes two encounter checks during the travel day.

4. If the group reaches a destination they wish to explore, such as a dungeon or POI, then its time for Dungeon Turns!

5. The travel day ends when the group rests. When resting outdoors, follow the procedure for resting. Resting in the Miasma means you don’t regain Effort.

6. After resting outdoors, each human in the group makes a Mind RR to resist the Miasma.

## Travel Pace

At the start of each travel day, the PCs should set their travel pace as a group.

- **Slow:** The PCs travel carefully, attempting to avoid danger. They travel 1 hex today. The EN for travel and rest encounters today is 2. All tests related to travel roles gain an edge.

- **Normal:** The PCs travel at a normal pace. They travel 2 hexes today. The EN for travel and rest encounters today is 4.

- **Fast:** The PCs prioritize speed over being noticed. They travel 3 hexes today. The EN for travel and rest encounters today is 6, and test related to travel roles take a bane.

### Changing Pace

A group’s overland travel pace changes based on the speed of the slowest creature traveling with the group. If a creature rides a mount or a vehicle, then they use the mount or vehicle’s speed for this purpose instead.

- If the lowest speed in the group is 3 or lower, the group moves 1 fewer hexes during a travel day.

- If the lowest speed in the group is between 7 and 9, the group moves 1 more hex during a travel day.

- If the lowest speed in the group is 10 or more, the group moves 2 more hexes during a travel day.

### Rivers and Roads

Roads and rivers and other bodies of water can change the travel pace of a group.

- If the group follows a road for their entire travel day, they move 1 more hex.

- If the group crosses a river or other major body of water without the use of a bridge or water vehicle or moves upstream on a body of water, they move 1 fewer hexes during the travel day.

- If a group moves downstream on a body of water, they move 1 more hex during the travel day.

## Travel Roles

Each day you travel, you can assign PCs and hirelings to the following roles.

- **Supporters:** Supporters help other roles do their job more efficiently.

- **Guide:** The guide leads the group to their intended destination. Only one creature can have this role at a time.

- **Scout:** The scouts keep an eye out for danger, shelter, and treasures.

- **Tracker:** Trackers search for food or attempt to find specific creatures.

Only one creature can take on the guide and scout role at a time. The supporter and tracker roles can have any number of creatures.

The creature assigned to the role decides if they want to take on a safe or risky task. If they play it safe, they provide a small benefit to the group and the travel procedure moves along swiftly. If they take a risk, they make a test, which could provide a greater benefit to the group or could make things harder for them.

Not everyone needs to take on a travel role. A person not in a travel role can make an assist to help an ally in a travel role undertaking a risky task.

### Supporter

In the supporter role, you take on one of the following tasks.

**Safe: Fight the Miasma** Choose an ally traveling with you. You engage them in conversation and entertain them to bolster their spirit. The next time that creature makes a Mind RR to resist the effects of the Miasma, they gain a +1 bonus to the test.

**Risky: Support Everyone** You attempt to aid several different allies in completing their roles. Choose up to three allies in travel roles today, then make the following test.

**2d10 + M or S**

**Tier 1:** The allies take a -1 penalty to tests related to their travel roles today.

**Tier 2:** The allies gain a +1 bonus on tests related to their travel roles today.

**Tier 3:** As tier 2, but bonus is +2.

### Guide

In the guide role, you can take on one of the following tasks.

**Safe: Normal Route** You take a straight forward path to your destination and prioritize speed. The group moves 1 more hex than the pace today.

**Risky: Secret Path** You attempt to take a secret route to your destination, one more guarded from hazards and monsters, and make this test.

**2d10 + A or M**

**Tier 1:** The group gets lost (see Lost).

**Tier 2:** The EN for travel encounters today is lowered by 1 today, but the group moves 1 hex slower than the pace you set today.

**Tier 3:** The EN for travel encounters today is lowered by 2.

**Risky: Shortcut** You attempt to shave time off your journey by taking a short cut and deviating from your path.

**2d10 + A or M**

**Tier 1:** The group gets lost (see Lost).

**Tier 2:** The group moves 1 hex faster than the pace you set today, but the EN for travel encounters raises by 1 today.

**Tier 3:** The group moves 2 hexes faster.

### Scout

In the scout role, you can take on one of the following tasks.

**Safe: Scout for Danger** You move ahead of the group and look for signs of potential danger to help the group avoid it. The EN for travel encounters lowers by 1 today.

**Safe: Scout for Places** You look for things worthy of being marked on your map. If a hex contains a POIs, dungeon, and villages that you didn’t previously know about, you find it. If you undertake this task, you can have a second scout in your group today undertake a different task.

**Risky: Scout for Shelter** You attempt to find a safe place to camp when traveling is done for the day.

**2d10 + A or M**

**Tier 1:** The Ref rolls an additional encounter check the next time you rest outdoors.

**Tier 2:** While resting outdoors today, the EN for travel encounters lowers by 1, but the EN for non-resting travel encounters increases by 1.

**Tier 3:** You find a ruin suitable for resting outside of the Miasma. There is a 10 percent chance this ruin is already inhabited with animals, monsters, or other humans (the Ref can use the encounter tables in *The Ref*).

**Risky: Treasure Hunt** You scour the land for treasures and objects that might aid you on future adventures.

**2d10 + M or S**

**Tier 1:** The Ref rolls an additional encounter check during the travel day.

**Tier 2:** The Ref makes a roll on the Minor Treasures table in *The Ref* to determine what you find, and rolls an additional encounter check while you travel today.

**Tier 3:** The Ref makes a roll on the Minor Treasures table in *The Ref* to determine what you find.

### Tracker

In the tracker role, you can take on one of the following tasks.

**Safe: Forage** You forage for edible vegetation and gain food equivalent to 1 ration.

**Risky: Hunt** You hunt edible game. Make the following test.

**2d10 + A or S**

**Tier 1:** The Ref rolls an additional encounter check during the travel day.

**Tier 2:** You gain 1d6 rations, and the EN for travel encounters raises by 1.

**Tier 3:** You procure 3d6 rations and 1d6 animal parts.

**Risky: Track Specific Creature** Choose a specific animal, such as a bear, or a monster, such as blood creature B that you want to encounter. You can also choose a specific, named human. If the Ref agrees that such a creature could be found on your journey today, make the following test. If not, choose another task or role.

**2d10 + A or M**

**Tier 1:** The Ref rolls an additional encounter check during the travel day.

**Tier 2:** You encounter a sign of the creature you wish to encounter, but need to go 1 hex off your path for the day to find them. The Ref determines which hex the creature is in.

**Tier 3:** You encounter the creature you wish to encounter. The Ref determines when and where this happens during the day.

## Lost

If the you get lost during travel, the Ref secretly keeps track of where you end up. While lost, a group doesn’t know their exact position in the world.

When you travel while lost, the Ref rolls 1d6 each time the group leaves a hex during the travel day and keeps the result a secret. Starting with the northernmost adjacent hex to the one the PCs are in, the Ref counts a number of hexes equal to the result going in a clockwise direction. The number rolled is the hex the PCs actually enter. If the result is a 1, the PCs move north. If it is a 2, they move northeast.

### Back on Track

The group can get back on track by finding a map, stumbling into a place they recognize that would allow them to get their bearings, or having an NPC provide directions. Otherwise, they need someone in the guide travel role to find their way. At the start of a travel day when lost, the guide can only undertake the following task.

**Risky: Back on Track** You attempt to become not lost.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** The group remains lost.

**Tier 2:** At the end of the day’s journey today, you realize where you are.

**Tier 3:** At the start of the day’s journey, you realize where you are.

## Miasma

Outdoor areas in Cornath are affected by a semitranslucent, magic haze called the Miasma, which appears a little different to all creatures. Your greatest fears always seem to linger within the effect. The Miasma is the result of the spells used during the Necromancer War. It lingers outside, but can’t enter indoor areas that are entirely enclosed in stone or metal. This phenomenon muddles the mind and corrupts the souls of humans, making them commit selfish and wicked acts. Too much time in the Miasma can create a permanent change.

When you finish a rest in the Miasma, you don’t regain any uses of your efforts, but all of the other normal effects of resting apply.

At the end of every rest a human spends within the Miasma, they must make the following test.

**2d10 + M**

**Tier 1:** The human gains a level of cruelty and must roll for a Miasma effect on the Miasma Effects table.

**Tier 2:** No effect.

**Tier 3:** The human removes all levels of cruelty they have, or they improve the test result of one other human who rested with them and made this test by a tier because they helped them resist the effects of the Miasma.

For each level of cruelty you have, you take a -1 penalty to RRs against the Miasma. You lose all levels of cruelty when you finish a rest in a location that has no Miasma.

When you roll on the Miasma Effects table, you roll 1d10 + your current cruelty level and gain both effects equal to your result. The second effect lasts as long as the first. If the result is a pair of effects that is already affecting you, then roll again for a different result.

**Miasma Effects**

| 3-4 | You become ravenous and greedy. You must eat at least 2 rations during a rest to get the benefits of a rest until you are out of the Miasma. | Your ravenous nature makes you good at finding food. You gain an edge on tests made related to the forage role. |
| 5-6 | You enter a destructive rage and destroy one mundane item randomly chosen by the Ref from your backpack or take 1 wound after punching a tree, wall, or other solid object. | Destroying something makes you feel good. You gain an edge on the next test you make. |
| 7-8 | You become secretly jealous of another PC of the Ref’s choice. If you provide them any help in the form of gifted or loaned equipment, assists or other test bonuses, magic or other effects, you take 1d6 P dam. This effect lasts until you no longer have cruelty. | When the PC takes damage, you gain an edge on the next test you make. |
| 9-10 | You become lazy. If you travel faster than a slow pace or move more than 2 squares during a combat turn, you take 1d6 P dam. This effect lasts until you are out of the Miasma. | When you rest, you recover 2 wounds instead of 1. |

### Merchants and Miasma

Traveling merchants brave the Miasma just like crows and for largely the same reason—to make money. Travelers who frequent the Miasma know that permanent effects take at least three consecutive days of exposure to set in, and plan their travel accordingly. One of the first purchases many merchants make once they have the coin is a *plague mask* to protect themselves further from the magical haze. They also hire a lot of guards, since monsters are afoot!

## Encounters and POIs

During overland travel, the Ref can describe the journey in a couple sentences as a simple montage. “You travel through the forest to reach your destination on a pleasant day. During the first few hours of your journey, the guide knows the way, the scout reports no trouble ahead, but the trackers find no food or game either.”

There are two instances the Ref should zoom in on the action. One is random encounters that occur during overland travel. The other is when points of interest (POIs), like unique landmarks, dungeons, and obstacles both mundane and magic, lie in the PCs’ path.

### Travel Encounters

Travel encounters are run-ins the PCs have with monsters, bandits, magic phenomenon, or other travelers on the road. The Ref should roll an encounter check after the role tests are done.

Travel encounter checks follow the same rules as the checks for dungeon encounters (see Dungeon Encounters), though the EN is determined by the group’s pace and their travel roles. The EN can never be lower than 1. The Ref can also adjust the EN based on nearby landmarks, such as increasing it by 1 if the PCs pass through a hex that includes a monster-infested dungeon.

If an encounter occurs, the Ref should roll on the encounter tables in *The Ref* to determine what the PCs encounter. The Ref decides at what point during the travel day the encounter occurs.

### POIs

POIs are landmarks significant enough for the Ref to zoom in on the travel and play out an exploration scene. These scenes should be run using DTs. If an encounter check occurs during the exploration of the POI, use the travel day’s EN for the check.

A POI should be an obstacle the PCs have to overcome to continue on their intended path, such as a river or chasm they need to cross, or a landmark they might wish to visit or investigate further. If the PCs decide to divert their path to avoid the obstacle or to not investigate the POI, that’s fine!

If the PCs spend more than 3 DTs investigating a POI in the middle of traveling, the Ref can determine that the distance they travel on the same day is 1 or more hexes shorter.

*Dungeons* has example POIs that the Ref can use or get inspired by to create their own.
