## Phonetic Construction

**Construction:**

- _Mnel-_ ← reduced from _mneme_ (memory), stripped to its consonantal core to give it that esoteric, half-lost feel
- _-yx-_ ← intrusive, non-etymological phoneme often emerging in dialect drift or ritual pronunciation
- _-phor-_ ← from _pherein_ (to carry/bear), but fossilized into a noun-form rather than a verb
- _-a_ ← discipline/force suffix (used for abstract practices rather than places)

**Implied Meaning:**  
“**That which carries memory**” or more obscurely, “**the bearing of impressions through time**”

---

## **In-world Meaning / Flavor**

Mnelyxiphora isn’t just “memory magic”—it’s the **manipulation of residual imprints left by existence itself**:

- Not only memories in minds, but **echoes in objects, places, and even events**
- Practitioners don’t “recall”—they **extract, distort, implant, or overwrite impressions**
- Strong users can:
    - Walk through a place and _experience layered pasts simultaneously_
    - Imprint false histories into people or locations
    - “Carry” a memory out of its origin, leaving behind a hollow absence

---

## **Common Misinterpretations (in-world)**

- Some scholars think it means _“memory theft”_
- Others translate it as _“echo-binding”_
- A forbidden interpretation: **“the migration of identity across time”**
---

## In the common tongue

Over time, cultures distort it further:

- **Nelyxora** (colloquial, simplified)
- **Mnelphora** (academic shorthand)
- **Ixiphora** (used by those who _fear naming memory directly_)
