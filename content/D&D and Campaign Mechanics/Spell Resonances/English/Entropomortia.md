# Entropomortia

## Summary

In combat, if you have this resonance, once per turn when you cast an Entropomortia spell, you can choose one visible creature within 30 feet or directly targeted by the spell. After the spell resolves and before your next turn, when an attack or spell would next deal damage to it, change one rolled damage die to 1 or its maximum for that creature.

## Resonance: Fate's Weight

*Time, fate, and the inevitability of death.*

Entropomortia arises from the will to challenge the natural order. Its practitioners weigh moments of harm, altering the consequences of what has already begun to unfold.

While you have resonance with Entropomortia, you gain the following benefit.

***Fate's Weight.*** Once per turn during combat, when you cast an Entropomortia spell, choose one creature you can see, including yourself. It must be within 30 feet of you or directly targeted by the spell, regardless of distance. The effect starts after the spell's initial effects resolve.

Before the start of your next turn, the next time an attack or spell would deal damage to that creature and rolls damage dice, choose one die after seeing the roll and change it to 1 or its maximum value for that creature. Do so before applying damage reductions from a save, Resistance, or Immunity, or increases from Vulnerability. The effect ends after that damage resolves or at the start of your next turn, even if the damage is later reduced to 0.

***Damage Rolls.*** An attack that misses or an effect that deals the creature no damage because of a successful saving throw doesn't trigger this benefit. Damage from a source other than an attack or spell and fixed damage without dice don't trigger it. If damage dice exist but the DM normally uses a listed damage value, roll the dice instead.

The change applies only to the chosen creature, even if the damage roll is shared. It keeps the die's damage type and is part of the original damage, not a separate instance.

This benefit needs no additional action, Reaction, or save. The spell needn't deal damage, require a save, or target the chosen creature; it works even if the spell misses or a target succeeds on its save. The spell's initial damage resolves before the benefit begins, but later damage from that spell can trigger it. Later effects don't activate the benefit again. It ends when combat ends.
