# Entropomortia
*Time, fate, and the inevitability of death.*

Entropomortia arises from the will to challenge the natural order. Its practitioners weigh moments of harm, altering the consequences of what has already begun to unfold.

## Resonance: Fate's Weight

While you have resonance with Entropomortia, you gain the following benefit.

***Fate's Weight.*** Once per turn during combat, when you cast an Entropomortia spell, you can choose one creature you can see, including yourself. The creature must be within 30 feet of you or be directly targeted by the spell, even if it is more than 30 feet away. Fate's Weight takes effect after the spell's initial effects resolve.

Before the start of your next turn, the next time an attack or a spell would deal damage to the chosen creature and one or more dice are rolled for that damage, choose one of those dice after seeing the roll. For the chosen creature, change that die's result to either the highest number on the die or 1. You make this choice before calculating the creature's damage, including any reduction from a successful saving throw, Resistance, or Immunity, or increase from Vulnerability.

Fate's Weight ends after that damage is resolved or at the start of your next turn, whichever comes first. The effect is used even if the damage is reduced to 0 after you change the die.

***Using the Benefit.*** Fate's Weight requires no additional action, Reaction, or saving throw. The Entropomortia spell needn't deal damage, require a saving throw, or target the chosen creature. You can use the benefit even if the spell misses or a creature succeeds on a saving throw against it.

Only the casting of the spell activates this benefit; its later effects don't activate it again. The spell's initial effects resolve before Fate's Weight takes effect, so the benefit can't alter their damage. It can apply to damage dealt later by the same spell.

***Damage Rolls.*** Fate's Weight changes only the damage dealt to the chosen creature, even if the same damage roll is used for multiple creatures. The chosen die retains its damage type, and the altered damage is part of the original attack or spell, not a separate instance of damage.

An attack that misses, or an effect that deals no damage to the chosen creature because of a successful saving throw, doesn't trigger Fate's Weight. Damage from a source that is neither an attack nor a spell doesn't trigger it.

Fate's Weight can't change fixed damage that has no damage dice. If an attack or spell has damage dice but the DM normally uses a listed damage value without rolling them, the DM rolls the dice for that damage instead.

## Combat Only

All resonance effects function only during combat. You can't use Fate's Weight outside combat, and its effect ends when combat ends.
